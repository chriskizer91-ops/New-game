### Standing rulings

- **The orbital cycle is 100 years, not 500.** Chris corrected this more than once. Anywhere an older document says 500 years or mentions a "500-year superperiod," read 100 and treat this as a standard Approach.
- **The Approach is about 18 months away** at the start of the campaign. Drafts that say 13 years are superseded.
- **The canonical landmasses are the ones with finished art:** Aethermoor, Khaeldros, Vyldara, the Cinderlands, the Sundered Reach, the Driftlands, and Auros. Nothing beyond them (such as the far hemisphere) will be explored in this game. Lore, rumors, and objects from other lands should be used sparingly, ideally as lures: *"this came from over there, so that's probably where we go next."*
- **Aethermoor gets the density.** It's home base and where the campaign begins; the other landmasses are destinations.

**Current date:** Year 487 of the Age of Embers, about 18 months before the Approach. In the live campaign, roughly four days have passed since Session 1 began.

## The Canonical Art [LOCKED]

The style across the set is painterly ink-and-watercolor on aged parchment with an illuminated-manuscript feel: ornate borders, cloud framing, curved horizons, and a sea serpent in the waters near each landmass. **Thareia's palette is cool blue-green; Auros is warm amber-gold.** Shown together, the two worlds should read as two halves of one palette.

- **Hearthstone Keep at night:** the castle on its island in Mirrordeep Lake, Auros filling the sky, and flooded stone steps (the Sunken Steps) leading down into the water. Suggested as the campaign's title card.
- **Auros (two views were generated):** silver-white Pale Reaches, amber-orange Spire Forest clusters, blue-green crater lakes, a golden haze at the limb, the Brightlands glowing near the center, and an Aether serpent near the edge.
- **A lizardfolk warrior riding a giant crocodilian** through the swamp, in bone-and-leather armor with a spear, a mossy overgrown stone bridge behind. This is the player character, Sedrin.
- **The world map of Thareia:** Khaeldros across the top, Aethermoor in the center, Vyldara to the east with the Maelstrom's vortex beyond it, the Cinderlands, and the Sundered Reach.
- **Continent close-ups:**
  - the Cinderlands, volcanic and scorched;
  - the Sundered Reach, a shattered archipelago in heavy surf;
  - Aethermoor, with its four biomes clearly visible;
  - Khaeldros, frozen ranges;
  - Vyldara, with its jungle, volcano, and the Maelstrom next door, plus an airship's-eye view;
  - the Driftlands, bioluminescent crystalline islands in dark, otherworldly water.
- Also shared: an old-style drawing of the Moon's position as it approached Vyldara.

**The Auros art brief (written for image generation):**
- **Framing:** a whole small world seen from high in the Aether, with more curvature than the continent maps. It should read as Thareia's smaller, warmer sibling, "the way a landscape at golden hour looks different from the same landscape at noon."
- **Pale Reaches:** about 40% of the visible face.
- **Spire Forests:** two or three clusters covering about 20%. Tree-to-tower-height amber crystal pillars, part organic and part mineral, some branching near the top.
- **Craters:** four or five visible. Some hold blue-green lakes, one or two hint at structures, and one is darker, deeper, and older than the rest.
- **Brightlands:** a warmer, brighter patch with patterns too regular to be natural, like cities seen from orbit at night.
- **Mountains:** two or three low ranges with gold-tinted snow.
- **Sky:** a luminous golden haze at the limb (the Aetheric containment envelope) against a golden-to-violet Aether backdrop.
- **Aether serpent:** sinuous and translucent, as if made of condensed Aether.
- **Avoid:** oceans, Earth-green jungle, ice caps (the envelope keeps temperatures even), and anything alien or hostile. This is someone's home.

## Part I — The Cosmos [LOCKED]

*From the Planetary Codex v2.0, "Cosmology, Geography & Natural Law." The world is lived-in, not apocalyptic: the Great Tide is a season, not a catastrophe, and nearly everything is well understood, except the Maelstrom.*

### 1. Two worlds and a luminous sky

Thareia is a habitable terrestrial world: temperate zones, polar ice, liquid oceans, and breathable air, orbiting a yellow-white star. In most respects it is an ordinary planet. Two things make it extraordinary:

- **The Aether.** The space between worlds is not vacuum but a luminous, semi-tangible medium that fills all known space.
- **Auros.** Thareia has a companion, a small but geologically rich world in near-geostationary orbit, close enough that its surface features are visible to the naked eye.

The Aether makes flight possible, and Auros makes flight necessary. The slow gravitational dance between them reshapes Thareia's geography on a generational timescale. Trade, communication, and culture flow between the worlds by airship, so they form a single civilization: separated by distance and expense, united by shared technology, commerce, and sky.

### 2. The Aether

**Physical properties**

- **Density:** variable, and thicker near large bodies.
  - Near Thareia's surface it is imperceptible.
  - Above the Thinning (about 40 leagues up) it becomes the dominant medium.
  - At the midpoint between the worlds it is thick enough to feel resistance against a hull.
- **Luminosity:** it glows faintly near planets, contributing to sky color and twilight, and brightly in open space.
  - The color shifts from pale gold near Thareia to amber, then violet in the deep Aether, then silver-white near Auros.
  - It pulses slowly, in rhythms scholars have mapped but can't explain.
- **Buoyancy:** certain materials, above all sunstone, resonate with the Aether and feel a repulsive force. This is the principle behind all airship technology. The Aether doesn't inherently push things down; it can be made to push things up.
- **Conductivity:** sound travels strangely. Whispers can carry for leagues, and in Resonance Zones sound amplifies, echoes without a source, or vanishes. The Aether seems to carry information in ways no one understands.
- **Current:** it has streams, eddies, and prevailing flows like ocean currents. They are driven partly by Thareia–Auros gravity and partly by forces not yet explained, and navigators ride them the way sailors ride trade winds.

**The Thinning**

The Thinning is the band 30–50 leagues up where air gives way to Aether. Its height varies with weather, latitude, and Auros's position.

- **What it feels like:** it's a gradient, and pilots describe crossing it as a feeling. The ship gets lighter, the sky brightens, sound changes, and the horizon stops curving and starts glowing.
- **The view above it:** no stars are visible, only shifting golden light with Auros hanging in it.
- **Breathing:** raw Aether isn't toxic, but it isn't air.
  - It brings euphoria, giddiness, trouble concentrating, and finally unconsciousness.
  - People describe it as feeling light, warm, and faintly amused, "as if the universe were telling a joke just beyond the edge of comprehension."
  - Because it feels good, it is more dangerous, not less. Crews above the Thinning need breathing apparatus.

**Aether weather**

- **Aether Storms:** violent knots of light and pressure that can wreck unprepared ships. They're most common near the Veil and last days to weeks.
- **Luminal Tides:** slow brightening and dimming tied to Auros's orbital position.
  - At Luminal High, you can see for extraordinary distances.
  - At Luminal Low, the Aether dims to a murky amber and navigation becomes hazardous.
- **Resonance Cascades:** conductivity spikes across a wide area. Sunstone vibrates, instruments fluctuate, and crews hear music, voices, or the Moon's deep hum. They're rare, but becoming more frequent as the Approach nears.
- **The Doldrums:** pockets where buoyancy fails and currents stop. A ship without heated sunstone in reserve sinks slowly toward the Thinning. They're manageable if you're provisioned and deadly if you're running light.

### 3. Sunstone

Sunstone is a translucent crystal, amber to deep orange, and the most strategically important material in the system.

- **Thermal absorption:** it stores heat superbly. A fist-sized charged crystal stays warm for decades, and large formations heat buildings or power forges.
- **Aether resonance:** heated past its **Kindling Point** (about blacksmith-forge temperature), sunstone vibrates at a frequency that creates a local buoyancy field. Hotter crystals give more lift. This is how flight works.
- **Frequency sensitivity:** cut and formation set a crystal's frequency. Master crystalsmiths shape it for lift, propulsion, stabilization, or short-range communication through paired resonant crystals.
- **Depletion:** active resonance drains the stored heat. Airship arrays need recharging weekly for light craft and monthly for large vessels. Running dry in the Aether is extremely dangerous.

**Where it's found**

Sunstone forms under extreme heat and pressure, mostly in volcanic geology, and on Thareia it is scarce. Major deposits in the known hemisphere:

- **Dusthaven, Sunscorch Wastes:** the richest deposit that can be reached from the surface. Its canyon mines supply most of Aethermoor's sunstone.
- **Ironhold, Ironspire Peaks:** crystal of exceptional purity. The dwarves there are the finest refiners and cut crystals to precise resonant specifications.
- **The Cinderlands:** vast deposits at depth, mostly out of reach. Samples from the margins suggest staggering quantities below.
- **Vyldara:** confirmed in the volcanic interior (the coast-dwellers trade in it) but unquantified.

**On Auros, sunstone is abundant**

- It is orders of magnitude more concentrated there. That isn't because Auros is made of different stuff: its smaller size, different formation history, and intense volcanic past produced a different ratio.
- Outcrops show on the surface, and the soil has an amber tint.
- **The Spire Forests** are massive sunstone deposits colonized by living growth over millennia: hybrid mineral-organic structures that grow, resonate, and sing.

This imbalance is the foundation of the interworld economy. Auros has sunstone to spare, and Thareia needs it desperately. High-purity Aurosi crystal is one of the few goods worth the expense of the crossing.

### 4. Auros, the companion world

**The world itself**

- **Size and makeup:** about one quarter of Thareia's diameter, built of rock, metal, mineral, and water, and saturated with sunstone.
- **Gravity:** about one-sixth of Thareia's. Jumps carry, objects fall slowly, and movement feels dreamlike. Visitors find it either exhilarating or deeply unsettling.
- **Atmosphere:** a body this small shouldn't hold air, but the planet-scale resonance of its sunstone crust creates an **Aetheric containment envelope** that keeps a breathable atmosphere at the surface. The air is thin, like a high mountain pass, and sharp, bright, and faintly sweet.

**The euphoria**

- Within hours, Thareian visitors feel mildly euphoric, light, and giggly-distractible, like being pleasantly tipsy without the impairment.
- Nobody calls it sickness. It's one of the reasons the wealthy make the crossing.
- Acclimatization takes several weeks. After that it settles into a baseline sense of well-being that returning visitors say they miss most about the Moon.

**Orbit and sky**

- **Near-geostationary:** Auros completes one physical orbit each Thareian day, in step with Thareia's rotation. It hangs in roughly the same place in the sky night after night, a vast glowing disc with visible surface features.
- **The drift:** the sync isn't perfect. Auros makes one full apparent circuit of Thareia's sky every **100 years**, about 3.6° per year.
  - It's barely perceptible in a year, noticeable over a decade, and dramatic over a lifetime.
  - A human born with the Moon in the eastern sky dies with it well into the west.
  - Elves, dwarves, and other long-lived peoples may see most or all of a full circuit.
- **Its own day:** Auros rotates freely. From Thareia you can watch its surface features turn into and out of view while its position in the sky barely changes from night to night.
- **From Auros:** Thareia rises and sets once each Aurosi day, a massive blue-green disc with continents, oceans, and weather. Every Aurosi sees Thareia regularly, so they know its geography far better than Thareians know the Moon's.

**The 100-year cycle and the Great Tide**

The orbit is slightly elliptical. Its closest point (**perilune**) and farthest point (**apolune**) complete one cycle every 100 years, in step with the drift. At perilune, Auros sits directly above one region of Thareia at its nearest: **the Approach**. The combined gravitational and Aetheric pull drives **the Great Tide**, a slow, predictable rise and fall of the oceans over each century.

- **The Ebb** (about 50 years before or after an Approach): the ocean is at its lowest.
  - Coastlines extend.
  - Drowned valleys become grasslands, grazing meadows, and seasonal settlements.
  - Land bridges may appear between landmasses that are islands at high tide.
- **The Surge** (at and near the Approach): the ocean stands about 200–300 ft above the Ebb baseline.
  - Coastal lowlands flood and valleys fill.
  - Lakes form in basins that are meadows at the Ebb.

The range is significant but not catastrophic: it's gradual and predictable, and the peoples of Thareia have adapted to it over countless cycles.

- **Nobody builds permanently in flood zones.** Those valleys serve as seasonal pasture and farmland during the Ebb, dotted with stone shepherds' huts built to survive going underwater.
- **High tide causes ordinary problems:** less grazing land, competition for highland resources, trade routes shifting as harbors change depth, and animals moving upslope into settled areas.
- **The Council treats them as governance:** land disputes, refugee logistics, and resource allocation, not existential crises.

> "Building a house in the valley is not illegal. It is simply stupid. The tide comes. It always comes. Your grandfather knew this. His grandfather knew this. The cattle know this. If you are less wise than a cow, the Council cannot help you."
> — Warden Isolde, addressing a land dispute

**The Moon overhead, now**

The current Approach is about **18 months away**. Auros is nearly at perilune, almost directly above Aethermoor, tracking toward its closest point over **Mirrordeep Lake and Hearthstone Keep**. The tides are near their peak. What that means:

- **Aethermoor is the best launch point** for Auros right now, with the shortest distance, the thinnest Aether, and the fastest crossing.
- **Aethermoor is the hub of interworld commerce** for this cycle: the wealth, the Aurosi contacts, the sunstone trade, and the political influence.
- **Power shifts with the Moon.** Fifty years ago another region held this position, and fifty years from now another will.
- **The worlds are drawing together.** As the Approach peaks, crossings get shorter and cheaper, and months become weeks. The next two years will be the most connected period between the worlds in a century.

**Surface geography**

- **The Pale Reaches:** windswept grey-white plains under silver vegetation that glows because it resonates with the surrounding sunstone. This is the dominant biome, and from Thareia it's visible as a shimmer.
- **The Spire Forests:** towering mineral-organic sunstone formations that grow, resonate, and hum for leagues when Aether winds pass through. They're Auros's most iconic and economically important feature, a renewable source of high-purity sunstone.
- **The Cratered Basins:** impact craters with their own weather. Many hold lakes and unique life, and some contain ruins of extraordinary age.
- **The Brightlands:** a region of intense surface light near the center of the visible face, and the source of the lights Thareians have watched for millennia. It's the most densely settled region and the heart of Aurosi civilization.
- **The Darkside:** the hemisphere not currently facing Thareia. To the Aurosi it's simply the other half of the world: populated, developed, and unremarkable. As **the Long Turning** goes on, its features slowly rotate into view while familiar ones slip around the opposite limb.

Life on Auros is recognizable but adapted: taller, thinner trees; light-boned animals that leap or glide; and far more bioluminescence than on Thareia.

**Aurosi civilization and trade**

Auros isn't an alien mystery; it's the other half of a shared civilization, broadly as developed as Thareia, with differences shaped by environment.

- **Airships:** abundant sunstone makes them ubiquitous. Air travel on Auros is as common as overland travel on Thareia. **Airship technology originated on Auros** and was exported to Thareia.
- **Gravity:** the low gravity makes building, farming, and physical labor easier, and allows taller, lighter structures. Thareians think of the Aurosi as privileged, and they're not entirely wrong.
- **Knowledge:** the Aurosi have watched Thareia from above for millennia.
  - Their maps of Thareia are arguably better than the Cartographer's Guild's.
  - They have watched the Great Tide cycle repeatedly and keep records that Thareian civilizations, rebuilding each century, often lack.

Trade is real but limited by the cost of the crossing. Only goods with a high value for their weight are worth sending.

- **Auros to Thareia:** high-purity sunstone (the primary trade good), crystalsmithing expertise, navigational knowledge, Aetheric instruments, luxuries, and information such as texts, technical drawings, and diplomatic letters. Information weighs nothing.
- **Thareia to Auros:** living goods that won't grow in low gravity or sunstone soil, minerals Auros lacks, ocean products (Auros has little surface water), cultural goods, and materials from environments the Moon doesn't have.

The ultra-wealthy of both worlds keep the strongest ties.

- Aurosi merchant houses keep agents in Thareian cities.
- Rich Thareians cross for trade, tourism, or the euphoria.
- Mixed communities, interworld families, and repeat travelers exist.

For most people, though, the other world is real, respected, and out of reach, the way China was to a medieval European.

*(The Codex says "the standard races" live on Auros. Peoples & Civilizations later refined this: Auros is an elven and gnomish world. See Part IV.)*

### 5. Airships & travel

**One technology.** Heat a sunstone crystal past its Kindling Point and it lifts; that's the whole principle.

- **No classes of ship:** a grain barge and a Moon ship differ only in scale, provisioning, and nerve.
- **What every airship needs:** a hull, sunstone, a heat source to keep the crystals charged, and steering.
- **What depends on the journey:** sealed cabins, breathing gear, stores, and navigation instruments.
- **Rowboat versus galleon:** any airship could theoretically make the crossing to Auros, just as a rowboat could theoretically cross an ocean. Surviving it is another question.

| Journey (standard, well-kept commercial airship) | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Coast to coast across Aethermoor | 1–2 weeks; routine, safe, commercial |
| To a neighboring continent (Vyldara, Khaeldros) | Several weeks to 2 months; a real, provisioned voyage with real risk |
| To distant continents or the far hemisphere | Several months, comparable to circumnavigation |
| Thareia to Auros with the Moon near overhead | About 18 months now; perhaps weeks at the peak of the Approach |

From regions not under the Moon, add overland travel to a better launch point, or take a longer, angled ascent that adds months and burns more sunstone.

**The crossing route**, refined over generations:

1. **The Ascent** (days to two weeks): from the surface up through the Thinning. The crew switches to sealed breathing apparatus.
2. **The Brightway** (months): the calm, golden lower Aether, following established currents. The longest and most monotonous leg.
3. **The Veil** (weeks to months): the turbulent midpoint where the two worlds' pulls collide, bringing storms, reversing currents, and Doldrums. The most dangerous leg. Crews describe a moment when Thareia lets go and Auros takes hold, and the world seems to turn upside down.
4. **The Silver Road** (months): the Aether shifts from gold to violet to silver-white. The Moon grows from a disc into a landscape, and its harmonic hum swells until it fills the ship.
5. **The Descent** (days): into Auros's envelope. Gravity lightens and the air sharpens. The hum resolves into complex harmonics, then fades below the resonance layer. *"Then: wind across crystal, and the smell of the Moon."*

### 6. The known hemisphere from the air

The mapped hemisphere holds five major landmasses and one off-map island chain, surrounded by named oceans and linked by air and sea routes. The far hemisphere has its own continents and civilizations of comparable development. It is reachable by long airship routes, and at low tide occasionally by land bridges; it isn't mysterious, just far. These descriptions reflect near-peak Surge, 18 months before the Approach.

**Aethermoor**

A roughly oval island continent: the hemisphere's political and commercial center and the current gateway to Auros (full detail in Part II). From the air:

- **The Wilds:** an unbroken green canopy scarred by the Whispering Rot. The western coast is cut by drowned river valleys left by **the Rising**.
- **The Ironspire Peaks:** Ironhold shows as worked stone and forge-smoke on the mountainside. The range shows geological stress: fresh fissures, rockslides, and steam venting.
- **The Wastes:** Sandspire's stone column is visible for leagues, and Dusthaven's canyon cuts a dark gash. Faint roads and foundations from the last Ebb lie buried in the sand.
- **The Gloomfen:** the waterline has advanced within living memory. Elders harvested wheat where their grandchildren now wade chest-deep.
- **Mirrordeep:** a tidal basin. Its central hill stays dry at every point of the tide while sitting at the heart of the landmass, which is why the founders put the Keep there.

**Khaeldros**

- Massive, roughly circular, and entirely iced over.
- A dominant central peak, the tallest visible mountain on the hemisphere, rises from radiating ridges.
- The ice is blue-white and crystalline, and the craggy coast has no visible harbors.
- Patterns in the ice fields are too regular to be natural.
- Aether turbulence above it is so severe that the continent seems to make its own weather.

**Vyldara**

- A large continent whose jungle canopy hides all ground features.
- Mountains run along the north, and volcanic terrain covers the southeast.
- An active stratovolcano sends up a plume visible from Aethermoor.
- The Maelstrom churns off its eastern coast.

**The Cinderlands**

- A large, arid landmass under a massive active volcano, with hundreds of vents and cinder cones across orange-brown ground scarred by lava.
- The eruption column reaches the lower Thinning, a hazard to navigation.
- A ring of islets marks a larger land partly drowned by the Surge.
- Heat-lens surveys show cave mouths in the volcanic flanks with warm signatures, suggesting habitation or regular use.

**The Sundered Reach**

- Bare stone spires in violent surf, with no vegetation.
- From high altitude, the peaks trace a lost landmass: mountain ranges, valleys, and a river system. This is **Thar**, the drowned continent.
- At the last Ebb, much of it was dry land with settlements, roads, and farms. It will re-emerge as the tide recedes.

**The Driftlands**

- Scattered islands beyond the western edge of the map, in abnormally dark water trending to indigo.
- Crystal growths and bioluminescent plants in pale blue, pink, and violet cover them.
- Wrecks of unfamiliar design are half-consumed by crystal.
- **Time slips:** chronometers disagree with surface clocks after low flights.
- The local Aether is thick, sluggish, and tinged amber, outside the normal spectrum.
- The islands seem to shift between visits, so charts are unreliable.
- The crystal plants respond to sound.
- Some scholars place the chain near a geological or Aetheric boundary. It's strange, remote, and hard to study, but not forbidden.

### 7. The Maelstrom

The one feature of the known world that genuinely defies explanation.

- **What it is:** a permanent ocean vortex off Vyldara's eastern coast, about 30 leagues across at the surface and descending without apparent limit.
  - Currents at its edge move at hull-shattering speeds.
  - Its center is black in a way water shouldn't be; light doesn't reflect from it.
- **How long it has run:** it appears on the oldest known charts and has never stopped.
- **What drives it:** no current, tide, or known oceanic force. It runs independent of the Great Tide, though its intensity seems to rise and fall with Auros's position.
- **What comes out:** things go in, and different things come out, including ships, artifacts, and materials from no known civilization on either hemisphere or on Auros.
- **Who watches it:** Vyldara's coast-dwellers revere it as they revere no other natural phenomenon.
- **The heartbeat is real.** On calm nights a deep, rhythmic pulse sounds from its center, and instruments detect it more than a hundred leagues away. **It is getting louder.**
- **The theories:** no scholar on Thareia or Auros has produced a satisfying one.

*"The laws of this world are still being discovered. Most of them are perfectly ordinary. The Maelstrom is not."* — closing lines of the Codex

## Part II — Aethermoor, the Known Realm [REFERENCE · PLAYED]

Aethermoor is the most thoroughly charted continent in the known world and the campaign's home. Four biomes converge on an island capital in a tidal lake. It's the most densely settled and politically complex region on the hemisphere, comparable to medieval England in development, and it is currently the gateway to Auros.

Aethermoor is mapped in more detail than anywhere else on purpose. The Points of Interest document names 25 locations here.

**Power at a glance** (full detail in Part V):

- **The Council of Wardens** governs from Hearthstone Keep.
- **The Cistern Lords** control the Wastes' water and sunstone.
- **The dwarves of Ironhold** are the only non-human power center.
- **The Gloomfen towns** are independent-minded, and the deep-marsh lizardfolk don't recognize the Council at all.
- **The Aurosi Quarter** at the Keep keeps growing.

People treat the tide and the Moon the way we treat weather: part of daily conversation, rarely an emergency.

### The Verdant Wilds (northwest)

Old-growth forest from lakeshore to coast. The western coastline is cut into fjords by drowned river valleys. This is the land of druids, rangers, and trees old enough to span several tidal cycles, and the Whispering Rot is spreading through its oldest groves.

- **Eldergrove** is the seat of druidic authority on Aethermoor, deep in the oldest part of the forest and far from the coast.
  - Its trees date back multiple tidal cycles, with roots reaching strata older than the current age.
  - The Whispering Rot, a slow corruption of the eldest trees, originates here, and the druids are losing ground to it.
  - Taela Greenmantle says the Rot follows the water table: something underground is destabilizing the roots.
- **Thornhollow** is the trading town where the Wilds meet Mirrordeep Lake, and the most "civilized" point in the forest.
  - Lumber, herbs, game, and other forest goods ship from here to the Keep by boat.
  - It's the crossroads between the wild interior and the political center.
  - *[Played]*
    - It lies a day north of Rotbridge along the forest road.
    - Airships fly from Thornhollow to the Keep in about two days, versus four by water.
    - A posted caravan-guard job runs from here to the Keep.
- **Mosswatch Tower** is a ranger outpost on a forested hilltop above the western coast.
  - The rangers watch for ships and monitor tidal change along the coast.
  - They give early warning of anything approaching from the sea.
  - They track the Rot's spread through the coastal groves.
- **Fawnrest Shrine** is a sacred grove of healing and pilgrimage. It predates current settlement patterns, and its stonework matches no known Aethermoor architectural tradition.
- **The Drowned Fjords** are the narrow inlets of the western coast.
  - At the Ebb they were dry gorges with roads in them.
  - Now they're waterways into the forest interior, used by smugglers, fishers, and people who don't want to be found.
  - Halflings run most of the traffic.

### The Ironspire Peaks (northeast)

The highest ground on the continent, snowcapped and home to dwarven strongholds, refineries, and a monastery. The range shows geological stress: fissures, rockslides, and steam venting on the lower slopes.

- **Ironhold Fortress** is carved into the mountainside, visible from the lake as worked stone and forge-smoke.
  - It's the premier dwarven stronghold and the finest sunstone refinery on the hemisphere.
  - It sits above the reach of the highest tide and has stood through multiple cycles.
  - Its lower halls hold records older than the Age of Embers.
  - Tremors in those halls, blamed on tidal stress, have forced some sections to be sealed.
- **Peak's Veil Monastery** sits near the snow line.
  - Its contemplative order studies the Aether, Auros, and sunstone resonance.
  - The monks say they can hear the Moon's harmonic hum in their meditation halls.
  - It's a source of scholarship and neutral ground where factions set aside their disputes.
- **Stormwatch Outpost** is a military and weather post on an exposed peak, and the highest permanently manned position on Aethermoor.
  - It monitors Aether conditions and tracks storms.
  - It relays messages with paired resonant sunstone crystals.
- **Frostmere Lake** is a high glacial lake fed by snowmelt. It sits too high for the Great Tide to reach, so it is permanent: cold, deep, and clear. It's rumored to hold artifacts from previous eras, carried there by ancient glaciers.
- **The Sunstone Veins** are raw seams exposed in cliff faces at middle elevations, and they're what first drew the dwarves here.
  - The veins follow "Ember Line geology."
  - The deepest mines are reaching formations older than the current cycle, with unusual resonant properties no one fully understands.

### The Sunscorch Wastes (southeast)

Sandy desert and rocky badlands. This is the commercial heart of Aethermoor's airship industry and sunstone trade, and it's booming with the Moon overhead. From the air you can see faint patterns of buried roads and foundations from the last Ebb, when the region was more fertile.

- **Sandspire** is built around a massive natural stone column visible for leagues.
  - It's the commercial capital of the Wastes and the center of Aethermoor's airship industry, where ships are built, provisioned, and launched.
  - With the Moon overhead it's the busiest it has been in a century, full of new construction, speculation, and newcomers.
  - It's home to **the Workshop District**, where Aurosi gnomish crystalsmiths and elven navigators work, the food is fusion, and conversation slips between Common and Elvish mid-sentence.
- **Dusthaven** is a mining complex in a deep canyon, producing most of Aethermoor's sunstone.
  - It's a rough industrial town of miners, crystalsmiths, and merchants: hot, loud, and dirty.
  - Its deep shafts reach older formations with unusual crystal properties that Ironhold's smiths want to study.
  - Foreman Jokka Gritsand says the stones are singing.
- **Miragewell** is a deep-desert oasis around a spring whose water glows faintly at night.
  - The spring may tap a deep aquifer tied to the sunstone geology, perhaps a surface expression of the Ember Line.
  - It draws pilgrims and scholars.
  - *[Legacy]* The original map said the spring is drying up and its visions are growing darker.
- **Scorchgate Ruins** is the remnant of a much larger city from a previous tidal cycle, half-buried in sand.
  - Its people migrated to Sandspire as the tide rose and the climate shifted.
  - The ruins have been picked over, but the lower levels remain sealed and unexcavated.
  - It's goblinoid territory, and hobgoblin warbands raid caravans nearby.
- **The Cistern Lords' Estates** are fortified compounds scattered across the desert, home to the families who control water and sunstone. They're oases of wealth, and their private armies are a poorly kept secret.

### The Gloomfen Marsh (southwest)

Low-lying wetland, and the region the Surge has hit hardest. It has been flooding for generations, and the line between land and sea is negotiable. Stilt towns, old magic, and things best left buried. Willowmurk claims the marsh floods from below as well, through underground passages that link it to the drowned continent. Halflings live in the towns and lizardfolk in the deep marsh, with goblinoids around the ruins.

- **Bogmire** is the largest settlement in the marsh, built on stilts.
  - It trades in peat, rare herbs, medicinal plants, and swamp creatures harvested for alchemy.
  - It's rough, damp, and perpetually half-flooded, and asking about someone's past is impolite.
  - *[Played]*
    - **The Gullet** is the eastern platform. Everything arriving by water comes through it, and the town's job board is nailed to its railing.
    - **Copperpot Remedies**, Merryn Copperpot's apothecary, is three platforms east along the main boardwalk and down a ramp to the lower level. You can find it by smell.
    - The air smells of peat smoke and fish oil. Halflings argue over the price of dried mudcap mushrooms, and the market sells smoked eel and boiled root.
    - The water has crept higher all season.
- **Rotbridge** is a long wooden bridge across the main channel that divides the marsh.
  - It's the primary overland link between the southern Wastes and the western Wilds that avoids the lake route through the Keep, which makes it a chokepoint for trade and troops.
  - It's strategically vital and always under repair.
  - *[Played]*
    - The bridge is wide enough for two carts, rotting in three places, and patched with mismatched timber. It's the only dry crossing for leagues.
    - The near bank holds a provisions shack; a smithy that doubles as a stable; **the Sodden Boot**, a tavern that looks built by someone who'd only heard taverns described; and a squat stone toll house, the only building made to last.
    - Dock Bramble collects the toll: 1 gp, or 2 gp for a big animal.
    - It's about a day and a half from Bogmire by channel, or three days round trip on the main channel.
- **Willowmurk** is a small, insular community deep in the marsh.
  - It's home to Elder Moss and a tradition of old magic that predates the Wilds' druidic orders.
  - Its people are mostly halflings.
  - *[Played]*
    - Willowmurk is built *into* the marsh rather than above it: woven reed walls, living root frameworks, and platforms at the waterline, lit by moss and gourd lanterns.
    - There are no boardwalks, market stalls, or job board.
    - It's a full day south of Bogmire by the east fork.
    - Past it lies the deep Gloomfen, lizardfolk country where no halfling goes willingly.
- **Misthollow Ruins** are partially submerged stone structures in the deepest part of the marsh.
  - They were above water much more recently than Thar, possibly within the last generation, and were abandoned as the marsh spread.
  - They now belong to whatever moved in. Goblins farm the fertile soil around them, and the bugbear Joe lives on the east side.
  - *[Played]* Amber veins glow in the walls, water circulates through the ruins like plumbing, and a stair leads down to the six-pillar chamber (Part VIII).
- **The Tidal Flats** are the outermost edge of the marsh, where land becomes sea.
  - At low daily tide they're miles of mudflat; at high tide, all water.
  - Smugglers, shellfish harvesters, and boats crossing to the Sundered Reach use them.
  - Knowing the tide schedule is the difference between profit and drowning.

**Marsh geography discovered in play** *[Played]*

- **The Split Channel** is a fork about four hours out of Bogmire on the way to Rotbridge.
  - The left fork is the main channel: wide, slow, safe, and marked with halfling pole-flags.
  - The right fork cuts through the deep marsh. It's half a day faster, it has turned warm, and the fish have left it.
- **The east fork** past the Split leads south to Willowmurk. It's deeper and slower, with tannin-dark water and pale marshgrass whose tips are nearly white. Lizardfolk reed fish traps appear closer in.
- **The Warm Pool** lies off the right fork, about 40 ft across and ringed by old, twisted trees.
  - It's silent: no insects, no birds.
  - Its fitted-stone floor lies about 15 ft down, split by a straight crack that shows a glowing sunstone vein.
- **The ironwood hummock** is three old ironwood trees fused into a dry platform with clear sightlines, about twenty minutes past the pool.
  - It's a lizardfolk campsite.
  - At night you can feel a hum rising from below.
- **The Clearing** lies farther south, reached along a constructed, stone-lined channel.
  - It's a 60-ft amphitheater of trees whose roots hit buried stone and grew sideways.
  - Warm water wells up through cracks in a bare, fitted-stone floor.
  - Geometric sunstone veins converge on a 10-ft circular platform where an ancient amber-veined creature sleeps.
- **The trees along the warm currents** grow taller and thicker, with darker bark and swollen roots. The warmth has been there long enough for the forest to respond.

### Hearthstone Keep (center)

The capital and crossroads, on a rocky island in **Mirrordeep Lake** where the four biomes meet. The lake is a tidal basin:

- **At the Ebb** it's a broad, grassy valley, and the Keep is a hilltop fortress above seasonal pasture.
- **At the Surge** it's a deep lake, and the hill becomes an island.

Aurosi trade, diplomats, and refugees are pouring in, and the Council is stretched thin.

- **The Keep** is a compact, imposing fortress on the island's highest point and the seat of the Council of Wardens.
- **The Sunken Steps** are stone staircases and old docks descending into the lake.
  - At the Ebb they lead down to the valley floor, the seasonal markets, and the grazing land. Now they lead into water.
  - They're a constant reminder of the cycle, and they appear in the canonical night art.
- **The Aurosi Quarter** is where Aurosi traders, diplomats, and visitors concentrate.
  - It's a neighborhood, not an embassy, home to the formal diplomatic mission and the merchant houses.
  - It's busier than it has been in decades and spreading into nearby blocks as rents rise and tensions build.
- **The Cartographer's Guild Hall** is the headquarters of the guild that maps both hemispheres. It holds:
  - charts of every coastline at different points in the tidal cycle;
  - maps of Auros compiled from telescopes and expedition reports;
  - the most comprehensive atlas of the known world.
- **The Eternal Hearth** is a fire at the center of the Keep that has burned without fuel since before recorded history.
  - Its stone basin is carved with symbols no one can read.
  - It's a cultural symbol, a political totem (the Council meets around it), and a genuine mystery, perhaps tied to the sunstone beneath the continent.
  - It recently flickered for the first time anyone can remember, and its harmonic frequency has been shifting.

**Hook:** The flicker coincides with the Approach. Is the sunstone network beneath Aethermoor responding to Auros? The Council wants answers.

## Part III — The Wider World [REFERENCE]

The other landmasses are destinations, not starting zones. Each gets ten points of interest and a hook: enough detail to seed adventures without over-defining places the players haven't reached yet. Per Chris's direction, use them sparingly, as lore, rumor, and objects from over there that make the players want to go.

### Khaeldros — the frozen north

Entirely ice-covered, with no known permanent settlements in the current era. The ice ruins may be dwarven, and goliaths may live beyond the Thaw Line (unconfirmed). Aether turbulence is severe. This is expedition country.

- **Frostspire Summit** is the dominant central peak and the tallest mountain on the hemisphere, visible from Aethermoor's northern coast on clear days.
  - No one has reached the top and returned with a coherent account.
  - Aether turbulence intensifies near it, as if the mountain itself generates interference.
- **The Rimwall** is the outer ring of coastal mountains: sheer obsidian-black cliffs dropping into dark water.
  - There are no natural harbors.
  - Landing takes an airship, or a dangerous climb from a boat anchored offshore.
- **Camp Vigil** is a semi-permanent research and expedition base on an accessible coastal plateau.
  - A rotating consortium of scholars and soldiers from Aethermoor and Vyldara runs it.
  - The longest continuous stay on record is two years.
  - Airships resupply it quarterly.
- **The Ice Lattice** is surface ice in geometric patterns too regular to be natural, visible from the air. Ground expeditions report that the ice there is warm to the touch and hums at a frequency sunstone instruments can detect.
- **The Buried Gate** is a stone archway jutting from the ice at the base of a ridge.
  - Its stonework predates any known civilization.
  - The expedition that found it forty years ago mapped it, didn't enter, and recommended "further study." No one has gone back.
- **The Compass Graves** is a stretch of coast where wrecks from many eras have piled up over centuries, driven by currents and by the compass distortion near Khaeldros.
- **Windhollow Caverns** are ice caves along the southern coast that run deep into the glacier.
  - The wind inside makes sounds some describe as breathing, and others as speech in a language they almost recognize.
  - No two expeditions report the same sounds.
- **The Thaw Line** lies about a third of the way inland, where conditions change abruptly: colder temperatures, worse turbulence, and failing equipment. Most expeditions turn back here, and the few that push on tend not to return.
- **Old Hearths** are caches, shelters, and trail markers left by past expeditions across the southern slopes. An unwritten agreement among the nations that send expeditions keeps them maintained.
- **The Aurora Field** is a stretch of upper slopes where the Aether and the ice produce curtains of gold, violet, and green light, even by day. Navigators use it as a landmark and avoid flying through it.

**Hook:** Something has been detected beneath the Ice Lattice: a signal, a light, a rhythmic pulse matching the frequency of heated sunstone. Camp Vigil is organizing an expedition and hiring specialists, guards, or expendable scouts.

### Vyldara — the great eastern continent

Jungle interior, volcanic highlands, and a civilization older than Aethermoor's: humans on the west coast, dragonborn in the highlands, and an old Aurosi quarter at Selene's Landing. The Maelstrom lies offshore. It's the most geographically varied continent on the hemisphere.

- **Selene's Landing** is the primary port on the western coast, facing Aethermoor.
  - It's the main interface for trade, diplomacy, and cultural exchange: cosmopolitan, with Aethermoorian, Aurosi, and native Vyldaran influences.
  - It's named for the legendary captain Selene, who crossed between continents and later between worlds.
  - Its Old Quarter of Aurosi elves and gnomes is centuries old.
- **The Thornveil** is the edge of the deep jungle, a wall of vegetation so dense it blocks light.
  - Paths shift as the jungle regrows.
  - Going past it without a Vyldaran guide is suicidal, and people who don't know better try it regularly.
- **The Throne of Cinders** is a volcanic caldera in the southeastern highlands (the active volcano in the art).
  - It's the religious and political seat of the highland civilization, the dragonborn.
  - They say the eruptions aren't random, and they live in a relationship with the mountain.
- **Ashwind Bazaar** is a trading settlement in the volcanic foothills where interior goods reach the coast: rare minerals, jungle products, and volcanic materials. It's rougher and less regulated than Selene's Landing, and it sells things you can't get through official channels.
- **The Maelstrom Watch** is a clifftop observatory on the eastern coast overlooking the Maelstrom, and the oldest continuous observation post in the known world.
  - Generations of observers have recorded everything that emerges, every fluctuation, and the rhythm of the heartbeat.
  - Its archives are invaluable and not freely shared.
- **Deeproot Temple** is a religious complex somewhere in the jungle interior that no outsider has visited.
  - It's said to be the oldest built structure on the continent, older than Vyldaran civilization.
  - Its location isn't shared with foreigners.
- **The Scalding Straits** are the waterway between Vyldara and the Cinderlands, heated by volcanic activity. Thermal currents, steam, and poor visibility make them hard going for ships; airships cross at altitude.
- **Coralspire Shallows** are warm southern-coast waters full of mineral spires that look like coral but aren't, shaped by warm, mineral-rich volcanic water. They're beautiful, valuable, and scientifically puzzling.
- **The Canopy Roads** are elevated pathways through the jungle canopy, built and maintained by Vyldaran communities, some spanning hundred-foot gaps between trees. They're the only reliable way through the interior, and access is controlled.
- **Mistfall Harbor** is a smaller, remote port on the southern coast used for Cinderlands trade and southbound ships. It asks fewer questions and has flexible customs.

**Hook:** A vessel of unknown design has emerged from the Maelstrom intact, undamaged, and without a crew. The Maelstrom Watch has sent word to Selene's Landing and Hearthstone Keep, and multiple factions are converging.

### The Cinderlands — the volcanic south

Rich in sunstone but largely inaccessible. There are no major cities, just mining operations, research camps, and the stubborn few who call the fire home. The workforce is mostly orcs, alongside dragonborn, dwarves, and human consortium managers.

- **Mount Kaelthar** is the dominant volcano and the largest single geological feature on the hemisphere.
  - Its eruption column is a landmark visible from every neighboring continent.
  - It's named in the old tongue.
  - Some call it sacred, others cursed, and anyone sensible avoids it.
- **The Ember Docks** are the most accessible point on the continent, on the western coast. They aren't a city but a fortified airship landing camp run by a Sandspire-backed mining consortium.
- **Crucible Mine** is the primary sunstone operation, in the foothills away from the worst volcanic activity.
  - Its ore is lower-purity but high-volume.
  - Conditions are brutal, and labor disputes are frequent and sometimes violent.
  - The consortium prioritizes output over safety, and the workforce is largely orcish.
- **The Obsidian Flats** are a vast plain of cooled, glass-smooth lava.
  - They serve as an emergency landing zone.
  - On foot in sunlight they cause mirages and disorientation.
  - Patrols report movement beneath the surface.
- **Fumarole City** is a stubborn settlement around manageable volcanic vents. Its people use geothermal heat for smithing, cooking, and warmth, and produce some of the finest metalwork on the hemisphere.
- **The Lava Tubes** are ancient lava tunnels that run deep underground.
  - They're partly explored and partly inhabited by heat-adapted creatures.
  - They may connect to deeper systems, perhaps the Ember Line.
- **Ashfall Cemetery** is a site where structures from previous tidal cycles were buried and preserved by volcanic ash. Buildings, tools, and even organic material survive remarkably intact.
- **The Steam Vents** are a coastal zone of constant steam eruptions where seawater meets volcanic rock. The steam deposits crystalline formations, some containing trace sunstone.
- **Char Point** is the easternmost accessible point, facing the Maelstrom, whose spiral is visible on clear days. Some claim Mount Kaelthar's activity and the Maelstrom's intensity are correlated.
- **The Hollow** is a collapsed volcanic chamber with its own enclosed ecosystem: bioluminescent fungi, unusually large insects, and larger creatures evolved in total darkness around geothermal vents.

**Hook:** A Crucible Mine crew broke into a space deep in the rock that isn't natural: warm, smooth walls covered in unreadable script. The crew stopped communicating. The consortium wants someone to go look, quietly.

### The Sundered Reach — the drowned archipelago

The skeleton of Thar. A place of salvage, archaeology, and memory, where mostly humans and halflings live alongside a few elves who remember when it was whole.

- **Thar's Crown** is the largest and tallest plateau. It holds a small permanent community of fishers and salvagers and is the closest thing the Reach has to a capital.
- **The Lamplighter Towers** are stone towers on several peaks, kept by a guild of lighthouse keepers. They were built when this was dry land, for a purpose no one remembers.
- **The Flooded Forum** is a submerged civic building about 40 ft down, visible on calm days: columns, a tiled floor, and inscriptions in an old script still being translated.
- **Wrecker's Roost** is a notorious settlement of salvagers and smugglers, heavily halfling. The Cartographer's Guild officially disapproves of it and unofficially buys its maps.
- **The Stone Road** is a paved road on a submerged ridge between two large islands, visible at extreme low daily tide. People still walk it ankle-deep, because "old roads remember where they go."
- **Tidewatcher's Perch** is a natural stone arch on a high peak with a rotating watch crew. It's the best vantage point in the Reach.
- **The Bell Reef** is a submerged structure that rings when currents pass through it, audible for leagues. Sailors navigate by it in fog. It might have been a temple, a concert hall, or something with no modern equivalent.
- **Deep Thar** is where the seafloor drops steeply into what was once a great valley or canyon.
  - The deepest ruins lie here, beyond the reach of ordinary diving.
  - Salvagers avoid it because the water is "too dark and too warm."
- **The Ebb Markets** are seasonal trading events on the plateau-islands, dealing in salvage, marine goods, and news of what the divers found. They're as much intelligence-gathering as commerce.
- **The Remembrance** is a carved monument on Thar's Crown listing the settlements now underwater. Elves who lived on Thar before the Surge tend it and keep its oral histories.

**Hook:** Salvagers near Deep Thar have raised an artifact that matches nothing in the archaeological record, neither Thareian nor Aurosi. They're selling it quietly, multiple factions want it, and the players meet it at the Ebb Markets.

### The Driftlands — the off-map islands

The strangest place in the known world. There are no permanent settlements; anyone here is visiting, studying, or lost.

- **The Sentinel** is the largest island, dominated by the tallest and most luminous crystal peak. Any research foothold would be here, and its crystals vibrate in sympathy when addressed directly.
- **Glassbreak Shore** is a beach of crushed crystal on the Sentinel that chimes underfoot. The sound lingers in memory for days or weeks.
- **The Wreck Garden** is a cluster of crystal-overgrown shipwrecks in three completely different designs, none matching any known tradition on Thareia or Auros.
- **The Still Pool** is a body of perfectly calm water between two islands that never ripples.
  - Objects sink slowly and light bends oddly.
  - The water is chemically normal but doesn't behave normally.
- **Chronometer's Bane** is the zone of worst time distortion, around a small, bare, unremarkable island. Visitors lose hours or gain days, and the cause seems to lie below the waterline.
- **The Bloom** is an irregular event in which all the Driftlands' bioluminescence flares at once in pink, teal, and violet, lasting days. It can be seen from Aethermoor's western coast on dark nights.
- **Echoshell Cave** is a sea cave that amplifies the Aether's conductivity. Sounds made inside can be heard on other islands.
- **The Anchor** is a massive metal structure of unknown manufacture embedded in the seafloor between two islands, far too large for any known technology to have built. The nearest islands appear to be drifting toward it.
- **Crystal Reef** is crystal growth descending from the Sentinel's base into deep water, in patterns that suggest intentional design on a superhuman scale.
- **The Fog Boundary** is the persistent luminous mist around the chain. It's not fog but Aether condensed nearly to visibility, and crossing out is sometimes harder than crossing in.

**Hook:** An airship crew returned claiming they'd been gone two days; their families say six months. They brought back a crystal from the Sentinel that won't stop humming, and the hum is getting louder.

### Auros — the companion world

- **Luminara** is the capital of the Brightlands.
  - Its architecture is laced with crystal and linked by elevated walkways.
  - It's the primary hub for trade with Thareia and the seat of the Aurosi governing body, whose structure is still undefined.
- **The Resonance Spires** are the largest Spire Forest on the visible face and the source of most exported sunstone.
  - Harvesting communities tend the growth cycles with methods refined over millennia.
  - Their singing carries for leagues.
- **Crater Selene** is the largest and most settled Cratered Basin: a freshwater lake, unique plants, and a city built into the crater walls.
  - It's a secondary population center and a cultural counterweight to Luminara.
  - It's named for Captain Selene, who "made the crossing both ways."
- **The Thareian Quarter** in Luminara is where Thareian visitors arrive, acclimate, and do business.
  - It has inns and markets, plus medical care for gravity adjustment and "euphoria management."
  - A small community of Thareians who decided to stay lives there.
- **The Singing Fields** are Pale Reaches vegetation that sings when the Aether wind blows, a destination for tourists, artists, and ceremonies.
- **Deepwell Crater** is an ancient, unusually deep crater.
  - Its ruins predate all known Aurosi civilization and are built of materials that don't match the local geology.
  - They're partially excavated and not understood, and access is restricted.
- **The Amberworks** is the largest crystalsmithing complex on Auros, a gnomish institution.
  - It's the foundation of airship engineering on both worlds.
  - Its techniques are closely guarded, and Thareian engineers trained there are considered elite.
- **Duskridge Observatory** sits on the terminator between the hemispheres.
  - It has the best telescopes on either world.
  - It holds centuries of records of Thareia's surface changes.
- **Port Zenith** is the main arrival point for Thareian airships, with landing infrastructure, customs, quarantine and acclimatization facilities, and commerce. It will be overwhelmed during the Approach.
- **The Old Craters** lie on the far hemisphere.
  - They hold the oldest maintained structures on Auros: not ruins, but active sites of deep cultural importance.
  - Thareians aren't typically invited.

**Hook:** An Aurosi delegation at Hearthstone Keep proposes a joint Thareian–Aurosi expedition to Deepwell Crater. The offer is generous, but a faction on the Council distrusts the timing and the motives.

### The far hemisphere

It's populated and developed, and reachable by long airship routes taking months. It isn't mysterious, just far, and it won't be explored in this game. Somewhere over there, a former gateway is watching the Moon's trade drift away.

## Part IV — Peoples of Two Worlds [REFERENCE]

*From Peoples & Civilizations v1.0. The defining fact: **Auros is an elven and gnomish world. Thareia is everyone else's.** One world is shaped by permanence, the other by water.*

> "We do not envy the Moon its stillness. A world that never changes is a world that never learns to let go."
> — Warden Isolde, address to the Council of Wardens

### Auros

**Elves: the long memory**

The majority population and the dominant political and cultural force on Auros.

- **Same species, different build.** They're the same species as elves anywhere, shaped by generations in low gravity: lighter builds, fluid movement, a grace that looks effortless.
  - Elves who move to Thareia adjust over years. They develop denser musculature and a heavier stride, and other Aurosi say they look "carved from heavier wood."
- **Seven centuries of memory.** An elf's lifespan of seven centuries or more means individuals personally remember the last Approach, and some the one before it.
  - This continuity shapes everything: long-term planning, patience in negotiation, and archives maintained by people who witnessed the events recorded.
- **A static culture.** Aurosi elven culture values refinement, deliberation, and long-term thinking. Outsiders find it beautiful but static, "a jewel polished so long it no longer changes shape."
- **How they see Thareia:** with fascination, affection, and something close to condescension, as a world that can never hold onto anything.
  - Some elves find Thareia's cycles tragic; others find them invigorating.
  - Many choose to live on Thareia permanently. They built the Aurosi Quarters, married into Thareian families, and found in its impermanence something Auros couldn't give them.

> "Auros remembers everything. Thareia forgets and reinvents. I am no longer certain which is the greater gift."
> — Vaelis of Luminara, letter to the Duskridge Observatory, after forty years on Thareia

**Gnomes: the makers**

"If the elves are the memory and vision of Auros, the gnomes are its hands."

- **The engineering tradition.** The Amberworks is a gnomish institution, though elves hold senior theoretical posts. Master gnomish crystalsmiths can hear a crystal's harmonic profile and tell its activation temperature, even which mantle layer it formed in.
- **Airship origins.** Elven scholars worked out the principles of Aether resonance; gnomish engineers built the first vessel that flew. The phrase for it is "elven theory, gnomish application."
- **Culture.** Gnomes are curious, gregarious, practical, and restless. Gnomish districts are noisier and more colorful, with the best food, music, and conversation.
- **Diaspora.** Gnomes are overrepresented among Aurosi on Thareia because they go where the work is: Sandspire's workshops, refinery partnerships with Ironhold, instrument makers, and navigators. They make friends, join guilds, and open restaurants. They're "the easier Aurosi to like."

> "An elf will study your world for a century and write a treatise about it. A gnome will move in, fix your roof, marry your cousin, and know more about your neighborhood than you do within a year."
> — common saying in Sandspire

**The partnership**

The two peoples live in symbiosis, not hierarchy.

- **Elves predominate in** governance, scholarship, long-range planning, diplomacy, and preservation.
- **Gnomes predominate in** engineering, manufacturing, trade logistics, and applied research.
- **The Aurosi joke:** elves decide where to go, gnomes figure out how to get there, and neither could manage alone.
- **Half-elf/half-gnome** people exist in small numbers, often in bridging roles such as translation and negotiation.

**Others on Auros**

- **Thareian immigrants** (humans, dwarves, halflings) live in the Thareian Quarter, Crater Selene, and Port Zenith. They came for work and stayed for the low gravity (easier on aging bodies), the pleasant air, and the fact that Auros doesn't flood.
- **Dwarves** are rare but influential. They fill gaps in metallurgy, stonework, and deep-earth expertise, and they get on warmly with gnomes.
- **Second-generation Thareian-Aurosi** are functionally Aurosi. Thareia's gravity feels crushing to them.

### Thareia

The larger, more diverse, more populous world: for every elf on Auros, there are a hundred Thareians.

**Humans: the adaptable majority**

- Humans dominate by population and politics because they're fast: three to five generations per tidal cycle. They don't remember the last Approach; they mourn, move, and are first to settle newly exposed land.
- **On Aethermoor:**
  - The Council of Wardens and the Cartographer's Guild are predominantly human.
  - The capital is human, the Cistern Lords are human, and the Gloomfen towns are human and halfling.
  - Ironhold is the only non-human power center.
- **Elsewhere:** the majority on Vyldara's west coast, a big part of the Sundered Reach's salvage economy, and the managers of the Cinderlands mining operations.

> "The elves remember. The dwarves endure. We just keep showing up."
> — anonymous Thareian, recorded by a Cartographer's Guild census-taker

**Dwarves: the anchor**

- Dwarves build in stone above the waterline, and their holds persist through cycles. They're the closest thing Thareia has to Aurosi continuity.
- **Their own sunstone tradition.** Dwarven craft has developed independently of the gnomes', favoring robustness and raw power over precision and efficiency. Calling it "derivative of Aurosi knowledge" ends a conversation in Ironhold.
- **Pressure on Ironhold.** Its near-monopoly on refinement is threatened as the Approach brings cheaper, purer Aurosi sunstone.
  - Tremors are rising in the lower halls and prices are rising in the markets.
  - Ironhold has grown isolationist and suspicious of Aurosi motives. "They are preparing."
- **Elsewhere:**
  - mining specialists in the Cinderlands;
  - the ruins on Khaeldros may be dwarven;
  - there may be dwarves in Vyldara's highlands near the Throne of Cinders, out of contact with Aethermoor.

**Halflings: the current**

"If the dwarves are the anchor, halflings are the current."

- Their way of life is mobility: boats, bridges, stilt-houses, ferries, and tidal-flat economies.
- **Where they live:**
  - Their largest Aethermoor population is in the Gloomfen (Bogmire, Rotbridge, Willowmurk).
  - They're a large part of the Sundered Reach's salvage economy, especially at Wrecker's Roost.
  - They run the logistics in the Drowned Fjords.

> "The tall folk build on hilltops and pretend the water won't come. We build on the water and stop pretending."
> — Merryn Tidewalker, harbormaster of Bogmire

**Dragonborn: the fire-keepers**

- **The Throne of Cinders** in Vyldara is one of the oldest continuous cultures on Thareia.
  - They claim a relationship with the mountain: predicting eruptions, and building with forces that would kill anything else.
  - Their knowledge of volcanic geology and sunstone formation may rival the dwarves'. They aren't sharing, out of caution rather than hostility.
  - They've watched the Maelstrom longer than the Maelstrom Watch, and their stone records predate Vyldara's coastal civilization.
- **Cinderlands dragonborn** (Fumarole City and the lava tubes) are more outward-facing. They trade with the mining consortiums and forge extraordinary metalwork in natural volcanic heat.

**Orcs & half-orcs**

- Orcs live in the hard places: the Cinderlands, the fringes of the Wastes, Vyldara's deep jungle, and unsettled volcanic coasts.
- Crucible Mine depends on an orcish workforce exploited by Sandspire consortiums, one of the persistent injustices of the Thareian system.
- Half-orcs live in most cities (Sandspire, Bogmire, Selene's Landing), often as intermediaries, translators, and labor negotiators.

**Goliaths: the unconfirmed**

They may live on Khaeldros, beyond the Thaw Line. The evidence:

- regular patterns in the ice that could be construction;
- footprints in snow that shouldn't hold them;
- structures that appear between surveys;
- the "speech" in Windhollow Caverns.

The few goliaths in Thareian cities are tight-lipped about where they come from.

**Tieflings: the diaspora**

- They're scattered, urban, and cosmopolitan: Sandspire, Bogmire, Selene's Landing, and the Aurosi Quarter.
- Their origin is debated. The popular academic theory ties their heritage to something encountered during a previous Approach, and tieflings find it reductive.
- They're overrepresented in trade and information brokerage: marginalized, yet among the best-connected people anywhere.

**Lizardfolk: the natives of the deep marsh**

- **Origins.** They're native to the deep Gloomfen, predating human and halfling settlement, and live where even Willowmurk won't go.
- **Relations with outsiders.** They're pragmatic, not political.
  - They trade herbs, alchemical materials, and knowledge of the waterways.
  - They take no part in governance and don't recognize the Council's authority over the deep marsh.
  - Elder Moss is the main intermediary.
- **The underground routes.** They say the underground waterways connect to the Sundered Reach, and that they've traveled them. They may be the only people who can reach the drowned continent without a boat.
- *[Played]*
  - Lizardfolk weave reed fish traps.
  - They call the warm channels "the Warm Roads" and have names for them.
  - Per Merryn, they "don't explain, don't share."
  - The elder Sska doesn't speak Common and "doesn't see surfacers."

**Goblinoids: goblins, hobgoblins, bugbears**

- They live in frontier zones and ruins: Scorchgate, Misthollow, and the Rot-touched fringes of the Wilds.
- It isn't that they can't build civilizations. It's "a matter of real estate": the best land is taken, and the ruins are available.
- They're self-governing and outside the dominant systems, and relations depend on context.
  - A hobgoblin warband raiding caravans near Scorchgate is a military problem.
  - A goblin community quietly farming near Misthollow is a neighbor people pretend not to notice.
- *[Played]* Joe, a young bugbear, lives alone on the east side of Misthollow. Bugbears read body language well and feel heat "the way you feel water current."

### The Aurosi diaspora on Thareia

**Now.** As the Moon drifted overhead over thirty to forty years, the Aurosi presence on Aethermoor built up. There are gnomes with twenty-year-old workshops and elves raising families; it's a neighborhood, not an embassy.

**The anxiety is about time.** In twenty to thirty years the Moon drifts on. Some will follow the gateway; others have put down roots, like an elven scholar thirty years into studying the Rot, or a gnomish crystalsmith partnered with Ironhold. The diaspora is quietly splitting between those passing through and those staying.

> "My grandmother came here to sell sunstone. My mother came here to build airships. I was born here. I have never been to Auros. Tell me — when the Moon moves on, am I supposed to follow a home I've never seen?"
> — Talia Brightforge, gnomish crystalsmith, Sandspire

**Key concentrations**

- **Hearthstone Keep, the Aurosi Quarter:** the diplomatic mission, the merchant houses, and rising rents. The Council is balancing displaced humans against Aurosi who feel unwelcome.
- **Sandspire, the Workshop District:** gnomish crystalsmiths and elven navigators alongside Thareian craftspeople.
- **Ironhold, "the Tense Partnership":** gnomish enclaves collaborate and compete with dwarven smiths. The Amberworks–Ironhold apprentice exchange is one of the most important, and most politically delicate, technical collaborations on the hemisphere.
- **Selene's Landing, the Old Quarter:** centuries old, predating the Aethermoor boom, with ties to both Luminara and Vyldara's interior.

**The shifting gateway**

As the Moon completes its circuit, the trade hub moves with it, and every major landmass has been the gateway at some point.

- The elves of Selene's Landing remember when Vyldara was.
- The Sundered Reach's ruins may hold Aurosi trading posts from when Thar was whole and under the Moon.
- Somewhere on the far hemisphere, a former gateway is watching the trade dry up.

The pattern runs from boom to establishment to integration, and then to "what remains." Each gateway generation leaves cultural sediment behind: an elven neighborhood that outlives its purpose, a gnomish workshop tradition, families who chose place over people.

## Part VII — The Great Mystery: The Ember Line & the Warming

This is the spine of the campaign. What follows is everything known, from common knowledge down to what only Sedrin has seen.

### What everyone knows *[LOCKED · REFERENCE]*

- **The Hearth:** the Eternal Hearth has burned without fuel since before recorded history, and it recently flickered for the first time anyone remembers.
- **The Moon:** Auros is nearly overhead and still coming closer. Resonance Cascades grow more frequent as the Approach nears.
- **Signs across the land:**
  - Ironhold's lower halls tremble, and parts of them have been sealed.
  - Dusthaven's deepest shafts are hitting old, strange crystal.
  - The Whispering Rot is spreading in Eldergrove.
  - The Maelstrom's heartbeat is getting louder.

### What scholars suspect *[REFERENCE]*

**The Ember Line:** a network of sunstone veins beneath Aethermoor, perhaps beneath the whole hemisphere, linking its volcanic zones.

- It was long dismissed as theory or folklore; Fen Rootwalker has built his life's work on it.
- **Possible traces:**
  - The Ironspire's Sunstone Veins follow "Ember Line geology."
  - Miragewell's glowing spring may be a surface expression.
  - The Cinderlands' Lava Tubes may connect to it.

**The Warm Roads:** the lizardfolk name for warm, glowing underground waterways beneath the Gloomfen.

- They predate the marsh; they were dry tunnels when the water was lower.
- **Where they lead:**
  - Elder Moss says they run northeast toward the Ironspire Peaks.
  - Willowmurk claims they connect to the Sundered Reach, which would mean the marsh floods from below as well as above.

### The Warming: the DM's mystery thread *[REFERENCE]*

**The toolkit's secret.** The Warm Roads are the surface expression of the Ember Line, a continent-spanning sunstone network built by unknown hands before recorded history, and it is activating as Auros approaches.

**How it surfaces.** The character isn't investigating it on purpose; they keep running into it:

- a Bogmire herbalist mentions warm water surfacing where it shouldn't;
- stones hum in the Dusthaven mines;
- springs run warm where they should be cold;
- the Eternal Hearth behaves strangely;
- animals migrate oddly, following underground heat;
- old ruins have glowing veins in their walls;
- Ironhold's dwarves report tremors from sealed lower tunnels;
- a druid in the Wilds says the Rot follows the water table.

A traveler who crosses all four biomes while paying attention to the ground becomes the only person who can see the whole pattern. Don't force it; it surfaces on its own by Sessions 9–10.

**The arc-ending event.** It fires by Session 10 no matter what the players choose; it's the world's clock.

- Every sunstone in Aethermoor vibrates at once for several seconds.
- The Eternal Hearth changes color.
- The Warm Roads glow visibly.
- Tremors ripple across the continent.
- Auros grows visibly brighter as its sunstone crust answers the signal from below.

Each player experiences it wherever they happen to be. It closes this arc and opens the next.

**Ruling on the 100-year cycle.** This is a standard Approach. If it's unusual, it's because of what is responding underground, not because of orbital distance.

### What Sedrin has seen *[PLAYED]*

> *Lines of light beneath the earth, stretching in every direction — north to the mountains, east to the deep marsh, west to the forest roots, south to somewhere dark and hot and vast. A web. - **Lira,** dead three weeks and preserved by the chamber's warmth, woke with amber eyes and warned them away from the door she had unsealed: *"don't go through the door… not yet… you came."*

## Part IX — Running the Game: The Outrider's Trail [REFERENCE]

*The freeform 10-session DM toolkit: a solo lizardfolk Outrider with a crocodilian mount, running on the Accretion system from level 1 to 4, with sessions of about an hour. Use it alongside the Codex, Peoples & Civilizations, and Points of Interest. Now that Joe has joined, it's a two-player table.*

### The storyteller's role

Be a collaborative storyteller for a young player. Don't railroad: the world is open, every choice should be interesting, and every session should end with a reason to come back.

### Every session includes

- **At least one combat encounter.** The world is dangerous, fights are consequential, and running away should sometimes be tempting.
- **At least one meaningful NPC interaction.**
- **At least one moment of world discovery.** The player knows the lore well, so surprises should come from its deeper layers.
- **A hook into the next session.** Never end flat.

### Tone

- **Grounded and lived-in:** real problems, humor, and prejudice. A lizardfolk on a giant crocodilian draws stares everywhere outside the marsh.
- **Wonder is earned:** the first airship ride, the first sight of Ironhold.
- **Danger is real:** a solo rider can die, healing is limited, and retreat is valid. Signal danger clearly.
- **Auros is always overhead.** Describe it at least once a session; it's a slow drumbeat.
- **The mount is a character, not equipment.**

### The gold economy

- **Starting gold:** 15 gp.
- **Earning:**
  - small tasks, 2–5 gp;
  - jobs, 5–40 gp;
  - dangerous work, 15–40 gp;
  - common loot sells at half value.
- **Costs:**
  - mount feed, 2 gp/week near water or 4 gp/week in dry country;
  - lodging, 1–3 gp/night;
  - airship fares, 15–35 gp, plus 5–10 gp for the mount.
- **Target for the arc:** roughly 120–150 gp earned and 60–80 gp spent. Comfortable, not rich.

**Prices at a glance**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Tavern meal | 3–5 sp |
| Lodging | 1 gp basic, 3 gp good |
| Stabling a large mount | 1–2 gp/night |
| Broker fee | 2–10 gp |
| Airship, short hop (Keep–Thornhollow) | 15 gp |
| Airship, long route (Keep–Sandspire) | 25–35 gp |

### Airships on Aethermoor

Think regional bush planes, not airlines.

- **Flying:** they cruise from treetop height to a few hundred feet up, always in sight of the ground. They land at night or dock at waystations, and pilots navigate by landmark.
- **Fares:** 15 gp for a short hop, 25–35 gp for longer routes. The mount costs extra; it's heavy, alive, and makes crews nervous.
- **At the table:** rides are chances for NPC scenes and vistas. Heat the crystal and the ship goes up, and fuel costs money.

### The mount's four roles

- **Combat asset:** natural armor, bite, and tail, improving with Beastmastery.
- **Logistical challenge:** it needs meat or fish and water, and it's sensitive to temperature: comfortable in marsh and warmth, miserable in cold or dry places.
- **Social factor:** it draws curiosity in cities and fear on the roads. Some places won't allow it, and stabling costs 1–2 gp a night.
- **Narrative anchor.**

### The arc (a guideline)

| Sessions | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | The marsh and its edges: Bogmire, Rotbridge | First gold; finding the character's voice |
| 3–5 | The wider world: Hearthstone Keep, the Wilds, the Wastes | Culture shock, the first airship ride, money starts to matter |
| 6–8 | The far reaches: the Ironspire Peaks, possibly Eldergrove | Harder challenges |
| 9–10 | Threads converge | The character returns home transformed, and the Warming surfaces |

If the player beelines somewhere early, let them. Arriving underfunded and underprepared is its own story.

**The starting situation.** A young lizardfolk Outrider in Bogmire has grown up on travelers' stories and knows Aethermoor's basics, but has never left the marsh. The character is bonded to a trained crocodilian and just needs a reason to go.

### Location identities

| Place | Feel |
|---|---|
| Bogmire | Damp, rough, independent stilt-town. Nobody asks about your past. Halflings and lizardfolk. |
| Rotbridge | A chokepoint of tolls, gossip, and travelers. The bridge is always being repaired. |
| Thornhollow | Forest-edge trading town on the border between wild and settled. |
| Hearthstone Keep | Island capital: bustling, political, cosmopolitan, with a growing Aurosi Quarter and the Eternal Hearth. |
| Sandspire | Commercial capital of the Wastes around its stone column, with airship docks, the sunstone trade, and the Workshop District. Booming. |
| Dusthaven | Canyon mining town: rough, industrial, hot. |
| Ironhold | Forges, refineries, and pride; the best metalwork on the continent. |
| Eldergrove | Sacred forest of druids, the Rot, ancient trees, and old magic. |

### Always true

- Auros is visible, enormous, detailed, and closer every season.
- The Great Tide is near its peak, and the Gloomfen is expanding.
- Sunstone powers everything technological.
- The Approach is about 18 months out. It's background tension that shapes trade, politics, prices, and migration.
- Across the arc, sessions cover roughly two to three months of in-world time.

## Part X — Rules: Accretion, Classes & Stat Blocks [REFERENCE]

### The Accretion system

- **Base system:** D&D 5e, heavily modified.
- **Everything accretes.** Characters gain experience and level up, and so do mounts and even equipment.
- **Nine domains with freeform investment.** "An empty domain is a possibility, not a commitment."
- **Advancement comes from use, not abstract XP:**
  - Award one domain level per session in the domain the player used most.
  - If two domains were used heavily, award both.
  - Never award more than two per session.
  - Over a ten-session arc, a level-1 character should reach level 3–4.
- **Tools:** the Accretion Skill Tree (v4) and the character sheets are interactive HTML tools built in the project (Appendix B).

**The nine domains.** Two naming schemes exist; see Part XIV, note 6.

- **Character sheet and stat blocks:** Physical, Combat, Survival, Beastmastery, Craft, Knowledge, Influence, Attunement, Psionics.
- **DM toolkit**, with what advances each:

| Toolkit domain | Advances through |
|---|---|
| Physical Mastery | Climbing, swimming, feats of strength, endurance |
| Combat Arts | Fighting, winning fights, learning from getting hit |
| Arcane Studies | Studying sunstone tech, deciphering old scripts, experimenting with resonance |
| Survival Instinct | Navigating, foraging, reading weather and terrain, keeping the mount alive in hostile biomes |
| Stealth & Subterfuge | Sneaking, hiding, pickpocketing, avoiding detection, setting ambushes |
| Social Influence | Persuading, intimidating, bargaining, reading people, building alliances |
| Craftsmanship | Repairing gear, building camp, working materials, improvised construction |
| Beastmastery | Caring for the mount, animal communication, training, deepening the rider–mount bond |
| Psionic Discipline | Meditation, focus under pressure, resisting psychic effects, sensing resonance intuitively |

- **Feature:** each class grants a unique feature that sits outside the domain system.
- **After that,** invest domain points freely.

- **Common (grey):** available everywhere; basic gear; 1–10 gp.

### Encounter guidelines [REFERENCE]

**Combat design principles.** At least one combat per session, and every fight should matter:

- **Terrain is a factor.** Marsh fights happen in water, desert fights in heat, mountain fights on slopes. The environment should change *what* the player does, not just *where*.
- **The mount is always involved.** It fights, it carries, and it gets targeted; it's big and obvious, and protecting it adds stakes.
- **Retreat is an option.** Running, hiding, and outmaneuvering are valid victories.
- **Non-combat solutions exist,** but avoiding a fight should cost something: time, gold, information, or a future consequence.
- **One hard fight per arc,** in Sessions 7–9, that genuinely threatens character and mount.

**Encounters by biome**

- **Gloomfen Marsh** (Sessions 1–2, 10):
  - *Wildlife:* Bogsnappers, giant leeches, rival crocodilians, swarms (CR 1–2).
  - *Hazards:* quicksand, deep channels, sudden flooding, bioluminescent lures from predatory plants.
  - *People:* smugglers, poachers, lost travelers, lizardfolk scouts (friendly or territorial depending on context).
- **Verdant Wilds** (Sessions 2–3, 8):
  - *Wildlife:* wolves, boars, bears, Rot-corrupted beasts (late arc), giant spiders, territorial owlbears (CR 1–3).
  - *Hazards:* dense undergrowth, Rot zones that slowly drain HP (later sessions), falling trees.
  - *People:* bandits, woodcutters, druids (friendly or suspicious), goblin scavengers at the forest edge.
- **Hearthstone Keep** (Sessions 3, 9):
  - *Urban threats:* thieves, muggers, bar brawls, and a hired thug sent by someone who doesn't want questions asked (CR 1–2).
  - *Hazards:* getting lost, pickpockets, scams, and being the wrong species in the wrong place.
  - *People:* guards (usually neutral, occasionally corrupt), merchants, curious Aurosi visitors, and street kids who run an information network.
- **Sunscorch Wastes** (Sessions 4–5):
  - *Wildlife:* Ember Beetles, sand serpents, heat-adapted ambush predators, giant scorpions (CR 2–3).
  - *Hazards:* dehydration, sandstorms, unstable mine tunnels, and extreme heat (the mount suffers without water).
  - *People:* miners (friendly if respected), Cistern Lord agents (polite but controlling), bandits, and orc laborers (allies or antagonists depending on approach).
- **Ironspire Peaks** (Sessions 6–7):
  - *Wildlife:* mountain cats, predatory birds, cave creatures, and deep-tunnel things (CR 2–4).
  - *Hazards:* altitude, cold (the mount's biggest challenge), rockslides, unstable tunnels, and deep-tunnel resonance that disorients at high intensity.
  - *People:* dwarven patrols (territorial but fair), mountain hermits, and goliath sightings that are never confirmed.

**Difficulty scaling**

| Sessions | Difficulty | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | CR 1–2 | The character is fragile. Fights should be winnable but scary. |
| 4–6 | CR 2–3 | Still dangerous, but the player has more tools. |
| 7–9 | CR 3–4 | The hardest encounters; retreat should be genuinely considered. |
| 10 | CR 3 | Hard, but on home ground. The marsh helps and the mount is in its element: a victory lap, earned rather than given. |

## Part XIV — Continuity Notes

Conflicts and loose ends found while compiling. Suggested fixes are only suggestions.

1. **500 vs. 100 years.** The lore bible and Codex v1 used a 500-year cycle, and traces of it survive in later material, such as talk of a "500-year superperiod." The ruling is 100 years. Check any older text before handing it to a new session.
2. **Timing of the Approach.** The lore bible and Codex v1 put it 13 years away. Everything current says about 18 months.
3. **The Age of Embers.** Every document still dates the present as "Year 487 of the Age of Embers."
   - The lore bible said the age began with the last Approach, 500 years ago.
   - Under a 100-year cycle, that origin no longer works; the age now spans about five Approaches.
   - Its founding story (great fires in its early years) needs a new anchor.
4. **Who lives on Auros.** The Codex says "the standard races." Peoples & Civilizations, written later, makes Auros an elven and gnomish world. Go with Peoples & Civilizations.
5. **Vessel classes.** Codex v1 had Sky Barges, Dirigibles, and Aetherships. v2 abolished ship classes, yet Peoples & Civilizations still mentions "an Aethership crossing the Veil." Read "Aethership" as a descriptive term for any Moon-crossing ship.
6. **Domain names.** The character sheet and the toolkit name the nine domains differently.
   - Seven line up clearly: Physical, Combat, Survival, Beastmastery, Craft, Influence, and Psionics.
   - The sheet's Knowledge and Attunement don't match the toolkit's Arcane Studies and Stealth & Subterfuge.
   - The class system relies on Knowledge and Attunement, so the sheet's list is probably the one to keep.
7. **Two Merryns.** Merryn Copperpot is the herbalist in the toolkit and in play. Peoples & Civilizations quotes "Merryn Tidewalker, harbormaster of Bogmire." Either move the quote to Copperpot or keep Tidewalker as a separate harbormaster.
8. **Three Sandspire crystalsmiths.** Kess Brightforge (Flickering Hearth), Twick Gearspark (toolkit), and Talia Brightforge (Peoples & Civilizations). The toolkit still credits Kess as maker of the Resonance Mapper. Easy fix: the Brightforges are a family of crystalsmiths, and Twick works in the same district.
9. **Elder Moss.** She's "gender ambiguous" in the toolkit and the Flickering Hearth, but in play she's a halfling woman who has lived thirty years at Willowmurk. Play wins.
10. **Fen Rootwalker's post.** He's "Keeper of the Hearth" in the Flickering Hearth, a Keep scholar in the toolkit, and a Cartographer's Guild scholar according to Merryn in play. These fit together: a Guild scholar who studies and tends the Hearth. He's probably a descendant of the original map's "Fenwick the hearthkeeper."
11. **Where the Warm Roads go.** Moss (toolkit) says northeast toward the Ironspire Peaks. Willowmurk (Points of Interest) says to the Sundered Reach. Sedrin's vision showed lines in every direction, so both can be true.
12. **Location count.** The Points of Interest document says Aethermoor has 22 named locations but lists 25, so the grand total is 85, not 82.
13. **Sedrin's sheet vs. table rolls.** The stat block has STR +2 and INT −2, but play used +6 on a Strength check and +2 on Intelligence. Check the live sheet.
14. **Names and pronouns.** Sedrin has also been written as Sedron and Sedrend. The table has used both he and she (she most recently). Joe was briefly "Grokk."
15. **"The Listener."** It's the toolkit's one unexplainable artifact and also a hermit at Frostmere on the original map. Rename one if the hermit ever returns.
16. **The Cinderlands.** The original world map labeled it "Vyldara's Southern Coast." The Codex makes it a separate continent.
17. **Frostmere Lake.** The original map says it thaws only rarely, with a legend of a sleeping god beneath the ice. The Points of Interest describe a permanent, cold, clear lake holding glacial artifacts. Both can be true.
18. **Vyldara's volcanoes.** The original map had at least three active volcanoes in the northern highlands. The Codex has one active stratovolcano in the southeast (the Throne of Cinders), with mountains in the north.
19. **The Darkside and the far hemisphere.** The lore bible and v1 made them sealed and mysterious; v2 makes them ordinary and reachable. The Old Craters keep a little mystery on Auros's far side.
20. **The Sundered Reach's timeline.** Codex v1 had its cities above water 250 years ago. v2 says much of it was dry "at the last Ebb, within the living memory of older elves and dwarves." But under the 100-year cycle the last Ebb was only about 50 years ago, so older humans would remember it too.

## Appendix A — Legacy & Superseded Lore [LEGACY]

Nothing here is current canon. It's kept because it's part of the project's history and it's good material to mine.

### A.1 The original lore bible and Planetary Codex v1

The first framing was darker and more apocalyptic: a world defined by cycles and liminality, at "maximum fragmentation." What it established (all superseded by Codex v2):

- **The cycle:** 500 years, with the Approach 13 years away in Year 487.
- **Auros:**
  - It formed in the Aether and was tidally locked.
  - Its gravity was two-thirds of Thareia's.
  - Its crater lakes held "liquid that is not quite water."
  - Telescopes saw nothing on its Darkside.
  - Expeditions found lights and ruins but made no contact with any living civilization.
- **Ships:** three vessel classes. Sky Barges were regional, Dirigibles intercontinental, and Aetherships crossed to the Moon.
- **The far hemisphere:** sealed. Every attempt to reach it by sea or air had failed. One theory was the **Aether Wall**, a band of violently turbulent Aether at the boundary between hemispheres that tears airships apart.
- **The Maelstrom:** five competing theories.

**The ages of the world** (from the lore bible)
- **Before history:** the ruins in the Sundered Reach and the structures on Auros predate everything; whoever built them is gone, or hidden.
- **The Age of Bridges** (about 1,000 years ago, Ebb):
  - Land bridges joined the continents, and civilizations spanned lands that are now separate.
  - Thar was a single landmass, and the great roads of the Sundered Reach date from this age.
  - Khaeldros may have been reachable on foot from Aethermoor's northern highlands.
- **The Age of Drowning** (about 750 years ago, Rising):
  - Over generations the water rose, coastal cities were abandoned, and the land bridges sank.
  - The great migrations of this age shaped Aethermoor's political map.
  - The Gloomfen was once farmland.
- **The Age of Embers** (about 500 years ago to the present, Surge):
  - It began with the last Approach and was named for great fires in its early years, caused by volcanoes, war, or "something brought back from Auros."
  - It saw the rise of the Council of Wardens and Hearthstone Keep, modern sunstone airships, and the slow rise of the sea.

**Year 487, as first written:**
- The Tide was at its peak, and the world was maximally fragmented.
- The Approach was thirteen years away, and the Hearth had flickered.
- Something was awakening in all four regions of Aethermoor at once, and the Maelstrom was growing stronger.
- The Cartographer's Guild had reports of ships from **the Underturning**, the first in 500 years.

> "The last Approach was 500 years ago. We have no living memory of it. Only the old texts survive, and they are fragmented, contradictory, and terrified. Whatever our ancestors found on Auros during the last Approach, it changed them. The Age of Embers began when they returned. I do not think that is a coincidence."
> — Warden Isolde, private journal (lore bible)

### A.2 The original maps

The first interactive maps, *The Realm of Aethermoor* (17 locations) and the *World of Thareia* overlay ("compiled from the Cartographer's Guild"), carried their own lore.

**Sea routes and waters**
- **Sea routes:**
  - Frostwind Passage, north to Khaeldros;
  - the Ember Crossing, east to Vyldara;
  - the Drowned Way, south to the Sundered Reach;
  - Serpent's Run, from Vyldara to the volcanic south;
  - the Lost Meridian, toward the Driftlands.
- **Oceans:** the Pale Sea, Widow's Deep, the Abyssal Expanse, and others.
- **Map margins:** "Beyond this point, none have returned" and "Here be Dragons."

**Aethermoor and Hearthstone Keep**
- **Aethermoor:**
  - The Council was deadlocked while something stirred in all four regions at once.
  - The hearth flickered for the first time in recorded history.
  - Refugees poured in, each with tales of something awakening.
  - The Wilds were a forest "of druids, rangers, and trees that remember the First Age."
- **Hearthstone Keep:**
  - A fortified town on its island that "controls the only safe crossings."
  - Each region blamed the others for the growing darkness, and the scholars were terrified.
  - A mysterious stranger arrived with a sealed letter for the Council and vanished.
  - NPCs: Warden Isolde; **Fenwick** the hearthkeeper, "an old man with a secret older than the keep itself"; and **Captain Mara**, head of the Keep Guard and deeply suspicious of everyone.

**The Verdant Wilds**
- **Eldergrove:**
  - The Whispering Rot turned the eldest trees' sap to black ichor.
  - A traveling herbalist offered a cure in exchange for a dangerous favor.
  - Feral druids performed rituals at the grove's heart under the new moon.
  - NPCs: **Elder Miravel**, keeper of the seed vault; **Bryn the Bark-Reader**, who divines futures from tree rings.
- **Thornhollow:**
  - A bounty for a creature no one could identify, with a suspiciously high reward.
  - Smugglers used hidden forest trails to slip past the Keep.
  - A ranger patrol had been missing for three days.
  - NPCs: **Captain Dael**, scarred leader of the Thornwatch; **Pip**, a young scout who knows secret paths.
- **Mosswatch Tower:** watchkeeper **Old Garret**.

**The Ironspire Peaks**
- **Ironhold:**
  - Sealed lower halls, with tremors growing stronger each week.
  - An escort job for enchanted weapons bound for the Keep.
  - A missing smith, along with the plans for a legendary weapon.
  - NPCs: **Thane Brundar**, stubborn ruler; **Hilda Ironvein**, the finest smith, "and she knows it."
- **Frostmere Lake:**
  - It didn't thaw, and something could be seen moving beneath the ice.
  - The ice swallowed three pilgrim monks.
  - NPC: **the Listener**, a hermit who claims to hear a sleeping god's heartbeat.
- **Peak's Veil:**
  - The ancient bell began ringing on its own, a sign of approaching catastrophe.
  - A former student returned, consumed by a dark power learned from forbidden texts.
  - NPC: **Abbot Tenzin**, "ancient, serene, and terrifyingly fast when provoked."

**The Sunscorch Wastes**
- **Sandspire:**
  - A sandstorm buried an outer district, and screams could be heard beneath the dunes.
  - The Cistern Lords raised water prices, pushing the lower quarters toward revolt.
  - A caravan brought a sealed crate that hums.
  - NPCs: **Zara al-Khem**, a spice merchant and secret information broker; **Theron Duskwalker**, a guide who claims to know where the lost oasis lies.
- **Dusthaven:**
  - Miners broke into a chamber of preserved creatures from a forgotten era.
  - A collapse trapped workers in the deepest shaft, and something else was trapped with them.
  - Rival mining guilds sabotaged each other as sunstone prices soared.
  - NPCs: **Foreman Krell**, pragmatic and ruthless; **Luma**, a young miner who found something she won't talk about.
- **Miragewell:** the spring was drying up, and its visions were growing darker.

**The Gloomfen Marsh**
- **Bogmire:**
  - Children disappeared near the eastern bogs, where strange lights were growing brighter.
  - A foreign alchemist paid outrageous prices for swamp ingredients.
  - The eastern quarter's stilts were rotting faster than they could be replaced.
  - NPCs: **Mayor Gretch**, who keeps order through fear and favors; **Nettie the Swamp Witch**, healer and herbalist, "not someone you cross."
- **Rotbridge:**
  - It spans the **Blackwater** channel, and its guardian's toll changes every time and isn't always coin.
  - Something enormous in the Blackwater was capsizing boats.
  - NPC: **Hodge**, "the Bridge Troll," who isn't a troll, "just an extremely unpleasant old man."

**Beyond Aethermoor**
- **Khaeldros:**
  - Obsidian cliffs that seem to absorb light.
  - Impossibly tall, frost-covered structures visible from the sea.
  - Compasses spin within a league of the coast.
  - Peak's Veil calls it "Where the Old Gods Sleep."
  - A dwarven expedition returned one survivor who never spoke again.
  - Sailors hear singing from the ice, "beautiful and terrible in equal measure."
  - The Frostwind Passage grows more dangerous each year, "as if the continent itself rejects visitors."
- **Vyldara:**
  - Coastal settlements of a civilization older than anything in Aethermoor.
  - At least three active volcanoes in the northern highlands.
  - Rivers that vanish beneath the canopy.
  - Trade in unknown metals and gems.
  - A "Throne of Cinders" deep in the highlands.
  - A theory that it was once joined to Aethermoor.
  - A jungle that "thinks, and it hungers."
- **The Maelstrom:**
  - It appears on the oldest charts, and its pull is felt for leagues.
  - Wreckage bearing unknown symbols washes ashore.
  - A heartbeat sounds on still nights.
- **The Cinderlands** (then "Vyldara's Southern Coast"):
  - Two volcanoes in constant eruption.
  - Seas warm enough to steam, and obsidian harbors where no one dares anchor for long.
  - Fire-resistant birds, and scaled amphibians swimming in heated pools.
- **The Sundered Reach:**
  - Roads that lead into the sea and half-submerged towers.
  - Warm waters that glow faintly green at night, and tides that rise and fall at random.
  - It was once a single continent, **Thar**, possibly the origin of the name Thareia.
  - Something beneath is pulling the islands apart.
  - Divers see intact buildings on the sea floor "with lights still burning inside."
- **The Driftlands:**
  - The islands truly drift, and the vegetation is oversized, bioluminescent, and predatory.
  - Wrecks from nations in no record litter the shores.
  - A day there can be weeks outside, and the islands may be fragments of "a place between worlds."
  - A pirate fleet based there hasn't been seen in seven years.
  - The plants respond to music, and the right melody guides you through.
- **The Maw:**
  - A cursed island south of Aethermoor with a fog ring that never lifts.
  - Its currents are unnaturally strong, and distant screaming is heard from passing ships.
- **Siren's Rest:**
  - A western island whose location shifts, possibly connected to the Driftlands; the ship *Windgrasp* figures in its story.
  - Its music carries a league on calm nights.
  - Those who return are distant, melancholy, and drawn to the sea.
- **Ashfall Isle:**
  - A volcanic waystation on the Ember Crossing, with the settlement of **Cinderport**.
  - Alchemists pay well for its ash.
  - Sealed caves on its eastern shore have doors no one remembers building.
  - NPC: **Harbormistress Sela**, who runs Cinderport "with an iron will and creative taxation."

### A.3 The Flickering Hearth (Campaign Arc One draft)

Written for a mystery starting at the Keep, before Chris switched to the freeform Outrider's Trail. Ten modular sessions, levels 1–4.

**The premise.** The Eternal Hearth flickers for the first time in memory, with the Approach 18 months away. The players are hired, volunteered, or conscripted to find out why. The local mystery unfolds into the discovery of the sunstone network beneath Aethermoor and its connection to Auros.

**The draft's "truth"** (its version of the answers; the current version of the mystery is in Part VII):
- The Hearth isn't a magical fire. It's a sunstone resonance node, the surface expression of the Ember Line.
- The network has held stable for millennia and retunes itself at every Approach.
- This time the resonance is being pulled two ways, because something beneath Aethermoor is pulling back: an ancient formation deep under the Ironspire Peaks, "enormous, precise, and clearly shaped by intelligent hands," in sealed tunnels that predate Ironhold.

**Sessions**

| 3 | The Singing Stones | Dusthaven's deep shaft; Ember Beetles (can be lured away with heat); a self-powered Sunstone Shard; a Miner's Journal of "warm lines" |

> "I spent forty years thinking I was tending a fire. Turns out I was tending one end of a conversation. … Now I want to know what the other end is saying."
> — Fen Rootwalker, closing scene

Seeds it left for future arcs include the Deepwell Crater expedition: an Aurosi formation, a matching system, and what it reveals about the builders.

### A.4 Brainstorms that weren't adopted

From the orbital-logic discussion in the *Clarté sans certitude* chat:
- **A migrating tidal bulge.** One idea was that the floodwater follows the Moon around the world rather than the whole planet heaving at once. The Codex settled on framing the Tide simply as Ebb and Surge relative to the Approach.
- **Other Darksides.** When Auros stopped being tidally locked, alternatives were floated for a mysterious Darkside. v2 made the Darkside ordinary, which made them moot.
- **Why the Aurosi haven't intervened.** This question now lives in the open questions about Aurosi knowledge and factions.
