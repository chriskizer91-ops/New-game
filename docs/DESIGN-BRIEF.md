# AETHERMOOR: Hearth & Heirloom
*Design brief for discussion. Built from four competing concept pitches scored by a judging panel.*

---

## 1. The Pitch

**Logline.** The eternal hearth of Hearthstone Keep has flickered for the first time in recorded history. Every legendary item in Aethermoor is now in someone else's hands. A scorpion has a burning scimitar stuck in its tail. A swamp-beast wears the antler crown you want. A toll-keeper swings a cheating coin on a chain. You are a young Hearthwarden, and you take those legends back one fight at a time. Each one appears as a big, detailed pixel-art card, then shows up on your character everywhere you go.

**The hook: every legend has a holder.** No epic item in this game sits in a random chest. Each of the 160 legendary relics is being carried right now by a named enemy, beast or character.

1. **You see it on them first.** The relic glints on the enemy's sprite. Tap and hold it to see its card, greyed out and stamped *HELD BY: KHARZUL THE GLASS SCORPION*.
2. **You feel its power.** The holder uses the relic's attack against you, so you learn what it does by getting hit with it.
3. **You pry it loose.** Break the holder's grip and the relic clatters to the floor. The enemy loses its power on the spot.
4. **You see it and wear it.** The card reveal plays, you press Equip, and it appears on your sprite in battle, on the map and in your trophy hall.

The bad guys keep getting stronger, and their gear keeps getting better, so better loot walks toward you in the hands of the next enemy.

**How it's different:**
- **Not Pokemon.** You don't catch creatures. You take legendary gear off named enemies, and the item collection plays the part of the Pokedex.
- **Not Final Fantasy.** There are no crystals, summons or hidden steal rolls. It's a visible fight over one specific item you can see on the enemy.
- **Not Diablo.** There's no loot fountain. Nothing important is a random drop, and every epic item comes with a story about who you took it from.

## 2. How It Plays

**The first 30 minutes**

- **0–5 min.** You create your hero on a screen that looks like your Aethermoor character sheet. You roll 4d6 for your stats on screen and pick a homeland. Then the hearth gutters mid-Council and the torches go blue.
- **5–10 min.** Fenwick the hearthkeeper opens the reliquary and offers three dormant heirlooms:
  - **Hearthbrand**, a fire sword
  - **Stillwater Lance**, a frost spear
  - **Cairnmaul**, a stone hammer

  Tamsin Vale, Warden Isolde's cocky ward, grabs the one that beats yours.
- **Your first fight.** A Tallyman thief bolts from the vault with the Keep's Warden Seal on his belt, and you can see it on him. You roll a d20 on screen: *17 vs Guard 13, HIT.* You smash his grip, the Seal hits the floor and you grab it. The screen dims and your **first item card** paints in line by line with a two-note chime. You equip it, and a small silver seal appears on your sprite's chest.
- **10–25 min.** You head down the Hearth Road North into the Verdant Wilds. Random fights in the bramble go fast:
  - A cutpurse wearing a rusty knife drops that exact knife.
  - Then you spot a gold glint on the map. **Old Snag**, a boar with a ranger's hatchet buried in its hide, is your first *Relic-Bearer*.
  - Tap-hold it to see the greyed card of the **Thornsplitter Hatchet**.
  - Win the tug-of-war and the full gold-frame reveal plays with a bell arpeggio.
  - Its field power cuts through the enchanted thorn walls that blocked the road. You can see the loot on the enemy, you win it, and it opens the map.
- **25–35 min, your first boss.** At Thornhollow, Captain Dael's bounty board shows a beast nobody can identify. Pip the scout joins you. You track **Briarmaw** into its den:
  - It fights in three phases.
  - Its thorn-crown can be snapped off mid-fight, and it stops summoning when it breaks.
  - Beat it and you earn your **first Brand**. One coal of the hearth relights, and the world wakes up one notch.

**The core loop**
- **Every few minutes:** walk a route, spot a glinting holder, read its card, fight, pry the relic loose, see the card reveal, equip it and see it on your sprite. Its power changes your moves or opens a new path, and that path leads to stronger enemies.
- **Each 15–30 minute session (built for a phone):** take a bounty, hunt one holder, upgrade the relic at the forge, tick a Codex page closer to its reward, then save at a Hearthfire.
- **Across the game:** you win 8 Brands. Each one makes you stronger and makes the whole world's enemies gear up.

## 3. Stronger and Stronger Bad Guys

**Six tiers.** Each enemy shows an intent die over its head, and the die grows with its rank. The die also tells you what the enemy will do next ("9: Glasscutter, charging").

| Tier | Intent die | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Rabble | d6 | Cutpurses, sand-skinks, bog lurchers. They wear rags. They never level up, so you stay a god to them. |
| Veterans | d8 | Painted shields and iron helms, with 1–2 bonus traits. Always drop a piece they're visibly wearing. |
| Relic-Bearers | d12 | About 70 named elites that glint gold on the map. Each carries one legendary relic. |
| Champions | d20 | The 8 Brand bosses. They fight in 3 phases and wear 2–3 breakable pieces; each piece you snap off shuts down one of their powers and drops as loot. |
| The Sleepers & the Hollow Council | d20 + | 4 ancient world-bosses with relics grown into their bodies, and 4 corrupted Council Wardens fought back to back. |
| The Unsmith | Two d20s | The final boss (3 phases). |

**Elite traits ("Omens")** stack on stronger enemies. There are 6 at the start and 30 by the end:
- **Emberblooded:** its hits set you on fire.
- **Thornskinned:** reflects melee damage back at you.
- **Twinned:** splits into two at half health.
- **Frenzied:** acts twice when it's nearly dead.

**The world wakes up.** Each Brand you win pushes the **Waking** meter up a step. At every step, every enemy family in every region visibly re-gears. The same Thornhollow bandit goes from a leather cap, to an iron kettle-helm with a tempered knife, to a glowing blue runed helm. Better gear on them means better loot for you.

The world map shows both meters side by side as the **Hearth Clock**: your Brands and the world's Waking. You and the world grow stronger together, and you can watch it happen.

**Champions keep pace.** After the Verdant Wilds, Sunscorch, Ironspire and Gloomfen open in any order:
- The regions you save for later have tougher Champions, but their relics come off already upgraded.
- If you wreck the Tallymen's field forge in Ironhold, every Champion after that re-gears at half speed. That's a real strategic reason to pick one route over the other.

**The Ladder.** A wanted-poster board in the Keep's war room shows every villain as a silhouette. Scouting fills in their faces, their traits and a silhouette card of what they carry. Taunting letters arrive from the Unsmith after each Brand.

**Grudges.** If an elite wipes your party or makes you run:
- It earns a title ("Skarn the Twice-Fled"), gets stronger and stalks that route.
- Beating it pays out one rarity tier higher, stamped *Grudge settled*.
- Losing sets up a better prize. It is never a wall.

**The rival.** You fight Tamsin Vale 7 times, and his gear visibly escalates each time: a leather cap, then a kindled blade, then a corrupted relic bleeding violet-black. At Rotbridge he trades his starter to the Unsmith. At the finale he comes back to stand with you.

**Why it won't feel grindy:**
- You can see the prize on every elite, so every fight has a point.
- The main path is tuned so you never need to grind.
- Weaker enemies visibly run from you on the map, and walking into one is an instant win.
- Fast battle speed is always available.
- Stripping a boss's gear piece by piece is itself the reward.

## 4. Epic Loot

**Rarity tiers.** The card frame and the reveal sound get grander at each tier.

| Tier | Colour | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| Worn | Grey | Base stats only |
| Wrought | White | 1 bonus trait |
| Tempered | Green | 2 traits |
| Runed | Blue | 3 traits + a gem slot |
| Storied | Violet | Generated name, lore line and a minor power; drops *unidentified* (see below) |
| **Heirloom** | Gold | Hand-designed, named, a signature power, often a map power |
| **Regalia** | Teal | Set pieces |
| **Primal** | White flame | Endgame and world-boss relics |

- **Bonus traits (affixes).** About 40 of them, each with quality stars, so a perfect roll is something to chase. Prefixes are named for regions ("Thornwoven": regenerate 2 HP a turn). Suffixes are named for skill Domains ("of the Stalker": +1d8 against unaware foes).
- **Identify ritual.** A Storied drop arrives as a black silhouette card. You roll to identify it, and the art develops pixel by pixel.
- **Sets.** There are 9 sets of 3 pieces, and each piece is held by someone in a different region. For example, the **Thornwatch Regalia**:
  - 2 pieces: regrow 5% HP each turn.
  - 3 pieces: your party can never be ambushed.
- **Gem slots and forging.** Regional gems (Dusthaven Sunstone, Frostmere Glass, Bog Amber) go into slots on your gear, and you can see them set into both the card and the sprite. Hilda Ironvein's forge offers:
  - **Temper** +1 to +10 (the metal visibly brightens).
  - **Reroll** one trait.
  - **Salvage** unwanted gear.
  - At Craft level 15, **Signature Masterpiece**: design and name your own legend.
- **Awakening.** This is our answer to evolution. Heirlooms go from Dormant to Kindled to Awakened as you complete three deeds with them. The card is repainted, and your Domain path decides which way it branches.

**The Hearth Codex, your collection.** It holds **160 numbered relics plus a secret No. 000**:

| Kind | Count |
|---|---|
| Starters | 3 |
| Heirlooms | 112 |
| Set pieces | 27 |
| Paragon relics (for maxing a Domain) | 9 |
| Primal | 4 |
| Corrupted | 4 |
| The Worldforge | 1 |

Each entry gets three stamps: **Sighted** (seen on its holder), **Claimed** and **Awakened**. The Codex is laid out like a binder, and finishing a page gives a permanent party bonus, so no relic ever becomes worthless.

**Where loot comes from:**
- Whatever enemies are visibly wearing.
- Champion pieces you snap off.
- Holders you win over by talking, trading or contests.
- Relics frozen in ice or buried in ruins.
- Drake's treasure maps (one in three is fake and leads to a Mimic).
- Tallyman caravans carrying stolen relics.
- Tavern rumours.

**Three items worth drooling over**

**No. 031 CINDERFANG.** Heirloom scimitar (fire).
- *Held by:* Kharzul the Glass Scorpion, lodged in its tail for three centuries.
- *Stats:* 2d8 slashing + 1d6 fire, +2 DEX, crits on 19–20, acts sooner in the turn order, 2 gem slots.
- *Glasscutter:* one attack roll against a whole row of enemies, and each hit sets the target burning for 1d6 a turn.
- *Map power:* melts glassed dune-walls in the Sunscorch.
- *Awakens into* Sunmarrow (the fire spreads) or Glassline (always strikes first).
- *"Forged in Scorchgate to kill the dragon that burned it. It failed. It has been warm ever since."*

**No. 077 HODGE'S UNFAIR TOLL.** Heirloom charm, a clipped coin on a chain.
- *Held by:* Hodge, the not-actually-a-troll of Rotbridge. You win it by beating him at his best-of-three toll game (Persuasion, Deception and Intimidation rolls). You *can* fight him instead. It's a terrible idea.
- *Stats:* +2 CHA.
- *Toll Is Due:* at the start of each battle, the strongest enemy must pass a Charisma save or lose its first turn.
- *Map power:* summons Hodge's ferry at any Blackwater dock.
- *"Hodge charges what he likes. Now so do you."*

**No. P-02 HUSH, THE FROSTMERE HEARTBEAT.** Primal shield (frost), and sentient.
- *Held by:* the Sleeper beneath Frostmere's ice. The heartbeat the Listener has been hearing all along is this shield. It can only be pried loose on the beat.
- *Stats:* +4 Guard, resist frost, +20% max HP.
- *On the Beat:* every 4th turn, your whole party acts first.
- *Ultimate, Stillwater:* freezes every enemy for a round and shatters the chilled ones for 4d12.
- It sometimes speaks in the Listener's voice.
- *"It never stopped beating. It was waiting for someone to carry it."*

## 5. The Item Card, and Seeing It Equipped

**The card.** An item in your bag is a small 16×16 icon. On its card it becomes a **96×96 pixel-art portrait**: 36 times the detail, with shading, rim light, engravings and your gems set in. It's shown at crisp 3× on a phone (fills the screen width) and 4× on a laptop. From top to bottom the card shows:
- The Codex number (*No. 031 / 160*).
- The portrait, inside a rarity frame: iron rivets, then silver, then gold filigree, then living flame.
- The name and type line.
- The stats, with green and red arrows comparing it to what you're wearing now.
- The power, in its own box.
- Deed pips, gem slots and temper flames.
- A **provenance ribbon**: *"Pried from Kharzul the Glass Scorpion · Dusthaven Deep Shaft · Day 19."*
- One italic lore line.
- While it's equipped, a wax **"Borne by" seal** with the wearer's face.

Flip the card over to read its **Chronicle**: foes felled, the mightiest kill, and everyone who has carried it.

**The reveal:**
1. The battle freezes.
2. The relic spins where it landed and rises.
3. It unfolds into the card, and the portrait paints in line by line while a chord builds (one note for common gear, a bell-lit arpeggio for Heirlooms).
4. For a boss relic, the boss's own theme plays back in a major key, so the villain's music becomes yours.
5. The *CLAIMED* stamp slams down.
6. You choose **Equip on...**, which shows each hero wearing it live, or **To the Reliquary**.

You can save any card as a PNG, sized to print as a real playing card for your tabletop group.

**Seeing it equipped.** One art "seed" draws every item at all four sizes, so the sword on your back is visibly the sword on the card.
- **Overworld:** layered 16×24 sprites showing your weapon on your back, your helm, armour and cloak. Heirlooms glint, and Awakened relics give off ember motes.
- **Battle:** 48×48 sprites holding the real weapon, with an attack animation for each weapon type.
- **Ultimate:** a Legend Surge slams the relic's own card portrait across the screen.
- **Armory:** your hero on a pedestal at 4×. Tap any slot to open its card.
- **Reliquary:** a walkable trophy hall under the Keep, with a lit pedestal for each relic. Empty pedestals show a silhouette and a riddle.
- **The world notices:** Hilda critiques your blade by name, and guards ask whether that's really Cinderfang.

## 7. Battles

- **Party:** 4 heroes (you + 3 companions), plus an optional bonded beast (it wears its own barding). Portrait phone layout: enemies at the top, the turn-order ribbon in the middle, your party at the bottom, and a dice tray that pops up when you roll.
- **Turns:** a visible **Initiative Ribbon** shows the next 8 turns. Heavier weapons act later: a dagger is quick, a maul is slow. Your gear sets your tempo.
- **Dice you can see:** d20 + bonus vs the target's Guard, and damage dice tumble in the tray.
  - A natural 20 is a Legend Strike.
  - A **graze rule** means a near-miss still does half damage, so the d20 never feels cruel.
  - Enemies roll their saves in the open.
  - Fate tokens let you reroll any die.
- **Elements:** each of the 12 Aspects beats 2 and loses to 2. Your weapon carries the attack element and your armour carries the resistances, so you play the type chart through your loadout.
- **Statuses:** Burning, Chilled (three stacks and you're Frozen), Poisoned, Rotting, Staggered, Frightened, Charmed, Hexed. Helpful ones: Warded, Hasted, Regenerating.
- **Grip & Claim:** relics on enemies show a chain icon and a grip meter. Crushing blows, Disarm and psychic Wrench wear it down. At zero:
  - The relic drops and the holder loses its power.
  - It's yours when the battle ends. There's no coin-flip race for it.
  - If you kill the holder first, the relic shatters. Hilda can reforge it, so nothing is ever lost for good.
- **The one ultimate, Legend Surge:** each relic builds a gauge. When it's full, the relic's card crashes across the screen and its signature power fires.

**Example turn, against Kharzul.** Kharzul's die shows *"9: Glasscutter, charging."*
1. Bryn uses Analyze: Kharzul has stone chitin, is weak to Crush, and has 22 grip on the tail blade.
2. Your Hearthwarden swings Cairnmaul at the stinger: *d20 14 +5 = 19 vs Guard 16, HIT.* The 2d10+3 crush damage rolls 15, and stone beats fire, so the grip drops to 0.
3. Cinderfang clangs onto the sand. Kharzul's charged Glasscutter fizzles, and the holder drops from d20 to d12.
4. Pip finishes it. The reveal plays, and one tap later Cinderfang is on your hip.

## 8. Growth

- **Levels 1–50**, plus 10 more in the post-game. Levels give HP, stat increases every 4 levels, and an **Accretion Mark** to spend.
- **Your Accretion Domains, used exactly as written.** All 9 Domains run from level 1 to 20, with a Path choice at 3, specialisations at 7 and master techniques at 13. The Imprint Bonus (+2 up to +6) and Imprint DC work as on your sheet.
  - Using a Domain also feeds it a little XP.
  - You get roughly 72 marks, which is enough for 3 Domains at 20 and a fourth partway. Builds really specialise.
- **Loot and Domains feed each other:**
  - Some relics lend temporary Domain ranks, which can unlock a branch early.
  - Your Path decides how an Heirloom awakens.
  - Taking any Domain to 20 awards its **Paragon relic**: 9 Codex entries you can only earn by building differently.
- **Domains as map abilities.** Every lock has two keys, a relic power or a Domain skill:
  - **Thorn walls:** Thornsplitter Hatchet, or Physical Power L3.
  - **Dark canopy:** Sunstone Lantern, or Attunement light.
  - **Chasms:** Windstep Boots, or a Physical Speed long-jump.
  - **Willowmurk's safe paths:** Pip in the party, or the Survival Pathfinder skill.
  - **Illusions:** the Mirage Glass, or Knowledge Arcana.
  - **Blackwater:** Hodge's ferry, or Survival swimming.
  - **Guarded gates:** talk your way past (Influence), or fight.
- **Companions**, each with fixed Domain leanings and branches you choose:
  - Pip: Survival
  - Bryn the Bark-Reader: Knowledge
  - Luma of Dusthaven: Craft
  - Nettie the Swamp Witch: Attunement
  - Rook: Influence
  - Sister Alondra of Fawnrest: Attunement (healer)
  - Brother Kesh: Physical

## 9. The World & Story

| Act | Region | How you get in | Brands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Hearthstone Keep | — | — |
| I (guided) | Verdant Wilds | Thorn walls | 1–2 (Briarmaw, the Rotwarden) |
| II (open, any order) | Sunscorch Wastes | Dune-glass walls, or the caravan road from Sandspire | 2 of 3–8 |
| II (open, any order) | Ironspire Peaks | The rockslide pass, or Stormwatch's writ | 2 of 3–8 |
| II (open, any order) | Gloomfen Marsh | The Blackwater crossing, or Willowmurk's safe paths | 2 of 3–8 |
| III | The Council Hall, then the Hearth Below | All 8 Brands | Finale |

**Act I, the Whispering Rot.**
- Pip joins at Thornhollow.
- Old Garret's midnight lights at Mosswatch turn out to be Tallyman signals.
- At Fawnrest the white deer have fled, and Sister Alondra dreams of four Sleepers.
- In the Eldergrove Heartroot, the Rotwarden wears a smith-forged mask that turns the eldest trees' sap to ichor.

**Act II, the Four Wakings.**
- **Sunscorch:** Zara al-Khem's humming crate, Luma's secret sunstone heart, Kharzul in the Deep Shaft, and the Ashen Warden in the Scorchgate Vaults.
- **Ironspire:** the missing smith is **Harrow Ironvein, Hilda's twin**. He stole the plans for the Worldforge, and Thane Brundar sealed the lower halls to hide it. The bell at Peak's Veil rings for a fallen monk. Rook, a former Tallyman, lays out the plan at Stormwatch. Hush beats under Frostmere.
- **Gloomfen:** the Lantern Mother is taking Bogmire's children. Tamsin falls to the Unsmith on Rotbridge. The Blackwater Leviathan is sinking boats. The drowned choir sings under Misthollow.

**The truth (Fenwick's secret).** The hearth never burned wood. In the First Age a smith chained four Sleepers beneath the land as its fuel, and every legendary relic carries a spark of them. Fenwick is the last First-Age hearthkeeper, kept alive by his poker. Harrow means to melt every relic into the Worldforge and kill the Sleepers, ending the age of heroes.

**Act III.**
- The stranger's sealed letter held four corrupted "gifts" for the Council. You fight Elder Miravel, Cistern Lord Qasim, Thane Brundar and Mayor Gretch back to back, snapping each gift off to free its wearer.
- Then you go down to **the Unsmith**, in three phases. In phase 2 he wears relics you never claimed, capped at **6 stolen powers**, so a light collector still gets a fair fight.

**Endings.**
- **Rekindle:** re-chain the Sleepers.
- **Release:** free them.
- **Kindle Anew (the true ending):** needs No. 000 (Fenwick's poker), your own Masterpiece and 120 Codex entries. You feed the hearth a legend *you* forged instead.

**Post-game.**
- Every relic the Unsmith absorbed returns to the world on new holders.
- The Emberless Reach opens past Stormwatch.
- The Heat 1–10 ladder: each rung adds enemy levels and Omens.
- Rematches against Awakened Champions.
- The Sleepers at full power.
- The First Smith, who waits for anyone who fills all 160 entries.

## 10. The D&D Soul

- **Nothing is hidden.** Six ability scores, d20 against Guard, saves against your Imprint DC, advantage shown as two dice, and short and long rests.
- **Skill checks show their odds** before you commit ("Persuasion DC 14 · 60%"). A failure opens a different branch, never a dead end.
- **The in-game character screen is your Aethermoor sheet.** It can export your hero to that file, so the video-game character can sit at your real table.
- **Table Mode:** roll your real dice and tap in the result.
- **DM trickery on purpose:** a third of Drake's maps are fakes, Hodge's toll changes daily, and some holders only appear on foggy nights.
- **Sentient relics** have an Ego score and argue with whoever carries them.
- **"Previously in Aethermoor..."** recaps greet you when you come back.

**The optional AI Dungeon Master (the Hearthteller)** is off by default, and the game is complete without it.

It *would*:
- Voice free-text conversations with Hilda, Zara, Elder Moss and others, who know which relics you carry and what you've done with them.
- Play Rook in a free-text interrogation. The game checks which facts you pried out and unlocks the matching rumours.
- Write the legend lines added to a relic's card when it awakens.
- Write the Unsmith's taunting letters.
- Write bounty flavour, and names and lore for generated violet items.
- Write session recaps.

It would *never*:
- Set a number, a drop, a stat or a story flag. The game engine decides all of those.
- Block your progress, or be required for any Brand, relic or ending.
- Spoil anything you haven't reached.
- Run slowly: if it doesn't answer within 3 seconds, hand-written text plays instead.

## 11. Two Alternative Directions

**A. The Cinder Ladder (villain-first).** Eight Ember-Bearers are fought in any order after the first. Each one you beat makes the survivors stronger and visibly re-armed, so whoever you face last is the hardest fight in the game and drops its weapon at its best. Every beaten boss's finishing move joins your roster of ultimates. The villains are the loudest here, but most epic items sit in chests rather than on the villains.

**B. The Graven Hearthdie (dice-first).** Your d20 is built from your loot: each attuned relic burns its power onto one face. When Forgefall owns your 20, rolling a 20 flashes its card and fires its power. Enemy dice grow from d6 to d20 as the world darkens. It's the most D&D of the four pitches, but your best item's power fires only on a 5% roll. **We could add it later as an extra layer on top of normal item powers.**

## 12. Decisions (all settled)

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solo hero or party? | **Party of 4 in battle**: you plus 3 of 7 recruitable companions. Every drop is a "who gets it?" choice. |
| 2 | Art style | **The Loot Forge look.** SNES-era richness, code-drawn pixel art with 64px+ item card portraits (approved from the prototype). |
| 3 | When the party wipes | **Wake at the last Hearthfire, keep all gear, lose 10% of gold.** The enemy that beat you becomes a Grudge. |
| 4 | Grinding | **Optional but beneficial.** The story never requires it; grinding pays out in loot, gold, Codex progress and Domain XP. |
| 5 | Tone | **Warm epic with an edge.** Real stakes, with Hodge-style comedy on the side. |
| 6 | World structure | **Guided start, then as open as possible.** See "Open world rules" below. |
| 7 | AI Dungeon Master | **Optional extra, never required.** Every line it could write has hand-written fallback text, and it never touches numbers, drops or story flags. |

### Open world rules
- **Act I is guided:** the prologue at Hearthstone Keep and the Verdant Wilds (Brands 1–2) teach the systems.
- **After Act I, Sunscorch, Ironspire and Gloomfen all open at once,** each with at least two ways in. Take them in any order.
- **Enemy strength follows the world, not the region.** Each region has a base level, and the whole world gears up as the Waking rises with every Brand you earn. Whichever region you save for last is the hardest and pays the best, so no order is wrong.
- **Two keys for every lock.** Any obstacle can be passed with a relic's map power or a Domain skill, so no single item bottlenecks the map.
- **Optional hard areas are visible early** (a glinting Relic-Bearer far above your level). You can try them, and the Grudge system turns a loss into a better prize later.

## 13. Build Roadmap

Every milestone is a new version at the **same private link** and plays on both phone and laptop:
- The phone layout is designed at 360px wide first: a touch d-pad plus A/B buttons, with 44px touch targets.
- On laptop: arrows or WASD, plus Z/X/Enter.
- Saves are local, plus an export code you can move between devices.
- The whole game is one self-contained file. Your illustrated map is reused as the fast-travel screen.

| Milestone | What you get |
|---|---|
| **M0 Engine** | Renderer, touch and keyboard controls, tile maps, saves |

**What's in the M3 slice:**
- **Places:** Hearthstone Keep (hub), Eldergrove and Thornhollow, 1 Hearth Road and 3 wild routes, Mosswatch Tower and the Eldergrove Heartroot, and Fawnrest Shrine as a side area.
- **Enemies:** 18 enemy families at 2 gear tiers, 6 Omens, 4 Relic-Bearers.
- **Bosses:** Briarmaw and the Rotwarden, plus 1 Tamsin fight.
- **Loot:** 24 Codex relics (3 starters, 18 Heirlooms, the Thornwatch Regalia) and about 100 generated item types (8 bases × 3 materials × 4 rarities, with 12 traits).
- **Party:** you, Pip, Bryn and Sister Alondra, with 4 Domains up to level 7.
- **Forge:** Hilda visits as a travelling smith (Temper only).
- **The Ladder board**, with every villain shown as a silhouette.

**The full game:**
- 10 towns, 24 routes, 12 dungeons.
- About 240 visible enemy variants and 30 Omens.
- 8 Champions, 4 Sleepers, the Council and the Unsmith.
- You + 7 recruitable companions (4 in battle).
- 161 Codex relics.
- All 9 Domains up to level 20.
- About 25–35 hours to finish, and 15+ more to complete the Codex.
